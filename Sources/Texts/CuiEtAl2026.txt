## Abstract
Understanding the demographics of close-in planets is crucial for insights into exoplanet
formation and evolution. We present a detailed analysis of occurrence rates for close-in
(0.5–16 day) planets with radii between 2 and 20 R⊕ around FGK main-sequence stars. Our
study uses a comprehensive sample from four years of TESS Science Processing Operations
Center full-frame image data cross-matched with Gaia, analysed through our rigorous
detection, vetting, and validation pipeline. Using high-confidence planet candidates, we
apply a hierarchical Bayesian model to determine occurrence rates in the two-dimensional
orbital period-radius plane. Our results are presented using 10-by-10 bins across the
period-radius parameter space, offering unprecedented resolution and statistical precision.
We find an overall occurrence rate of 9.4 +0.7/−0.6 %. When using identical binning, our
occurrence rate posterior distributions align with Kepler's but have on average smaller
uncertainties. For hot Jupiters, we estimate the overall occurrence rate of 0.39 +0.03/−0.02 %.
This value is consistent with the previous Kepler FGK-type result within 1σ. We find an
overall occurrence rate of Neptunian desert planets of 0.08 ± 0.01%, to our knowledge the
first such determination. Additionally, in a volume-limited Gaia subsample within 100 pc
in the same parameter region, we measure an overall planet occurrence rate of 15.4 +1.6/−1.5%
and a hot Jupiter occurrence rate of 0.42 +0.16/−0.12%. Our results establish an improved
foundation for constraining theoretical models of exoplanet populations.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Close-in planet overall occurrence rate: 9.4% for FGK stars** (2–20 R⊕, 0.5–16 day
  period). This is the TESS-era update to earlier Kepler results; smaller uncertainties
  while consistent with Kepler posteriors.
- **Hot Jupiter occurrence rate: 0.39%** (FGK main-sequence stars); volume-limited
  sample within 100 pc: 0.42%. Consistent with Kepler within 1σ. This validates
  and tightens the hot Jupiter rate.
- **Neptunian desert occurrence rate: 0.08 ± 0.01%** — first quantitative determination
  from TESS. The Neptunian desert is the dearth of Neptune-sized planets at very short
  orbital periods (<3–4 days); this rate represents the rare planets that survive in
  this region despite intense atmospheric escape.
- **10×10 period-radius bins:** Unprecedented resolution in the period-radius parameter
  space; individual bin posteriors available from the Zenodo data release.
- **Volume-limited sample enhancements:** Within 100 pc, overall rate is 15.4% — higher
  than the magnitude-limited full sample (9.4%) due to bias corrections.
- **Hierarchical Bayesian model:** Properly propagates uncertainties from detection
  efficiency through to occurrence rates; avoids Simpson's paradox artifacts.
- **Limitation:** Study covers 2–20 R⊕ and 0.5–16 day periods only. Earth-sized planets
  (< 2 R⊕) and longer-period planets are not covered by this analysis.
- **Limitation:** FGK main-sequence stars only. M-dwarf occurrence rates require the
  GillisEtAl2026 companion study.
- **Limitation:** TESS geometric transit probability bias toward close-in planets is
  fully corrected, but contamination from false positives may remain at small levels
  despite the vetting pipeline.

**Neptunian desert survivor probability:**
```csharp
// Neptunian desert: very rare close-in Neptunes (survived photoevaporation)
// Apply only at orbital period < 4 days and 3–8 R⊕
bool inNeptunianDesert = planet.OrbitalPeriodDays < 4.0
    && planet.RadiusEarth >= 3.0 && planet.RadiusEarth <= 8.0;
double neptunianDesertSurvivorProbability = 0.0008;  // 0.08%
if (inNeptunianDesert && rng.NextDouble() > neptunianDesertSurvivorProbability)
{
    // Planet would be in desert but not a survivor — photoevaporation removes it
    planet.WasEvaporated = true;
    return;
}
```

**Related corpus papers:**

  and Gillis (M-dwarf) provide a complete TESS-era occurrence rate picture.
- VanZandtEtAl2025.txt — Distant giant survey around stars with inner small planets;
  complementary to Cui's close-in demographics (Cui = inner planets, VanZandt = outer).
- TanakaTakeuchiWard2002.txt — Planet migration model; hot Jupiters are the endpoint
  of Type II inward migration; Cui's 0.39% hot Jupiter rate calibrates migration outcomes.
- ForganRice2010.txt — Criterion 3 (no Jupiter-like inward migration = Good Jupiter);
  Cui's hot Jupiter rate directly sets the probability of this criterion being failed.

**Key inconsistency to watch:**
Cui et al. use 2–20 R⊕. Below 2 R⊕ (super-Earth/rocky planet regime) is not covered.
For Earth-twin occurrence rates around FGK stars, use Bryson et al. (2021) or the
broader FGK habitable zone occurrence rate literature.
