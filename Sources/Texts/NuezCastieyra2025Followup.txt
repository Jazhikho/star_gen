## Abstract
We investigate the role of baryonic physics in shaping the population, structure, and
internal dynamics of galactic subhalos using the Mochima suite of cosmological zoom-in
simulations. A refined method is developed to identify bound subhalo material by isolating
the local gravitational potential and applying multi-criteria phase-space selection. This
approach enables robust characterisation of subhalo properties across five baryonic runs
with varying prescriptions for star formation, and supernova and protostellar feedback,
as well as a dark matter-only baseline. At the population level, we find that host halo
concentration, modulated by baryonic feedback, is a key predictor of subhalo survival.
Subhalos with more massive stellar components exhibit deeper internal potentials and
enhanced resilience to tidal disruption. At the structural level, we identify a broad
diversity in inner dark matter profiles, consistent with observations of dwarf galaxies.
We show that this diversity correlates with both star formation history and environmental
interaction. In particular, galaxies that form most of their stars early tend to retain
steep cusps, while those with extended or recent star formation exhibit oscillating inner
slopes shaped by bursty feedback and tidal perturbations. These findings suggest that the
so-called "diversity problem" may reflect the complex interplay between feedback history
and gravitational environment, rather than a breakdown of cold dark matter predictions.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Host halo concentration predicts subhalo survival:** More concentrated host halos
  (shorter characteristic scale radius) destroy subhalos faster via tidal stripping.
  Baryonic feedback modulates the host's concentration — supernova feedback puffs up
  the host, reducing concentration, and thereby increasing subhalo survival rates.
- **Stellar mass protects subhalos:** Subhalos with more massive stellar components have
  deeper gravitational potential wells and are more resistant to tidal disruption. This
  explains why dwarf galaxies (stellar-rich) survive longer than dark-matter-only
  subhalos of the same total mass.
- **DM inner profile diversity = resolved diversity problem:** The observed variety in
  inner dark matter profiles (cuspy vs. cored) of dwarf galaxies is not a flaw in cold
  dark matter (CDM) — it reflects the diversity of star formation histories and tidal
  environments, both of which are physical and predictable.
- **Early star formation → cuspy DM profile:** Galaxies that form most of their stars
  early (before z ~ 2) retain the original cuspy NFW-like dark matter profile because
  supernova feedback episodes ended before they could flatten the cusp.
- **Late/extended star formation → cored DM profile:** Galaxies with extended or bursty
  recent star formation have oscillating inner slopes — supernova-driven gas outflows
  heat the DM cusp, potentially flattening it over multiple cycles.
- **Baryonic feedback prescriptions matter:** Different sub-grid models (supernova rate,
  protostellar feedback) produce measurably different subhalo populations and DM profiles,
  highlighting the model dependence of simulation predictions.
- **Limitation:** Zoom-in simulations (Mochima suite) focus on one galaxy halo and its
  subhalos; not a statistical sample. Generalization requires caution.
- **Limitation:** The "diversity problem" resolution is specific to their simulations'
  feedback prescriptions. Other feedback models may not produce the same profile diversity.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**DM inner profile by star formation history:**
```csharp
// DM inner profile classification (Nuñez-Castiñeyra et al. 2025)
DMProfile innerProfile;
if (galaxy.StarFormationHistory == SFH.EarlyBurst)
{
    innerProfile = DMProfile.Cuspy;    // NFW-like; cusp preserved
}
else if (galaxy.StarFormationHistory == SFH.ExtendedBursty)
{
    innerProfile = DMProfile.Cored;    // flattened by bursty SN feedback
}
else
{
    innerProfile = DMProfile.Intermediate;  // between cuspy and cored
}
galaxy.DarkMatterProfile = innerProfile;
```

**Subhalo survival fraction by host concentration:**
```csharp
// Higher host halo concentration → fewer surviving satellite subhalos
// Nuñez-Castiñeyra: survival fraction inversely correlated with concentration
double survivalFraction = Math.Max(0.1, 1.0 - 0.4 * galaxy.HaloConcentration / 10.0);
// galaxy.HaloConcentration: typical range 5–20 for spiral galaxies
// At c=10: ~60% survive; at c=15: ~40% survive
int survivingSatellites = (int)(galaxy.ExpectedSatelliteCount * survivalFraction);
```

**Satellite galaxy star formation type:**
```csharp
// Stellar-rich satellites are more resilient → they survive tidal stripping longer
// Affects satellite galaxy catalog properties in galactic generator
bool stellarRich = satellite.StellarMassFraction > 0.05;
if (stellarRich)
{
    satellite.TidalDisruptionRisk = TidalDisruptionRisk.Low;
}
else
{
    satellite.TidalDisruptionRisk = TidalDisruptionRisk.High;
}
```
