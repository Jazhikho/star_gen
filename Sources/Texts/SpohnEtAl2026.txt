## Abstract
This article reviews the emerging field of exo-geoscience, focusing on the geological and
geophysical processes thought to influence the evolution and (eu)habitability of rocky
exoplanets. We examine the possible roles of planetary interiors, tectonic regimes,
continental coverage, volatile cycling, magnetic fields, and atmospheric composition and
evolution in shaping long-term climate stability and biospheric potential. Comparisons with
Earth and other planets in the Solar System highlight the diversity of planetary conditions
and the rarity of conditions relevant to life. We also discuss contingency and convergence
in planetary and biological evolution as they relate to the spread of life in the universe.
The observational limits of current and planned missions are assessed, emphasizing the need
for models that connect internal dynamics to detectable atmospheric and surface signatures
as well as the need for laboratory measurements of planetary properties under a wide range
of conditions. The large number of exoplanets promises opportunities for empirical and
statistical studies of processes that may have occurred earlier in Earth's history, as well as
for the other pathways rocky planets and biospheres may take. Thus, exo-geoscience provides
a framework for interpreting exoplanet diversity and refining strategies for detecting life
beyond the Solar System.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations

**Plate Tectonics:**
- **Stagnant-lid is the natural default:** Convection in a fluid with strongly
  temperature-dependent viscosity naturally produces an immobile lid. Plate tectonics
  (mobile-lid with subduction) appears to be unique to Earth among present-day Solar
  System planets. Mars and Mercury show stagnant-lid; Venus likely stagnant-lid currently.
- **Mobile-lid tectonics history:** Mars and Venus may have had early mobile-lid or
  "squishy-lid" tectonics in their Archean analogs. The geochemical cycling effect (not
  the rigid plate geometry itself) is what matters for habitability.
- **Tectonic modes:** Heat-pipe → squishy-lid → plate tectonics represent an evolutionary
  sequence driven by planetary cooling. The transition is gradual, not sharp.
- **Plate tectonics importance for biosphere:** Critical for: (1) geochemical cycling
  between interior and surface reservoirs, (2) continental crust production, (3) nutrient
  delivery (P, N) via continental weathering and riverine transport, (4) atmosphere buffering
  via CO₂ drawdown in subduction zones, (5) coal formation (organic burial by subduction).

**Land-Ocean Balance:**
- **Continental crust coverage:** Earth's continental crust covers ~40% of total surface;
  ~29% is above sea level. This ratio has been roughly stable through geological history.
- **Optimal balance vs. likely outcome:** Roughly equal land/ocean maximizes net primary
  productivity (NPP) and nutrient availability. However, Höning and Spohn (2016, 2023)
  find that feedback in continental growth and water cycling makes this the LEAST LIKELY
  outcome:
  - Most likely: all-continental (mostly land) world (feedback drives continent growth)
  - Also common: all-ocean world (water cycling buries continental crust)
  - Least likely: balanced Earth-like land/ocean ratio
- **All-ocean or all-land worlds: NPP ~1% of Earth's.** O₂ productivity on all-ocean
  worlds may be insufficient to reach Balbi2023's 18% threshold.
- **Land biomass >> ocean biomass:** Land-based biomass exceeds oceanic by 2 orders of
  magnitude. Most life at the "complex life" level is phototrophic and land-dependent.

**Interior Structure and Magnetic Fields:**
- **Core radius ~0.5 planetary radius:** Most differentiated rocky planets share this
  ratio. Earth's magnetic field is generated by liquid outer core convection (buoyancy
  from inner core freezing). Without an active dynamo, atmosphere is exposed to stellar
  wind stripping.
- **Mass controls longevity:** Smaller planets (Mars, Mercury) lose heat faster, freeze
  their cores, and lose their dynamos early. Super-Earths may maintain dynamos longer
  but also have slower mantle convection rates, potentially suppressing plate tectonics.

**"Euhabitable" vs. habitable:**
- **Glaser et al. (2026) (this topical collection) introduce "euhabitable"** (truly
  habitable) as distinct from the classical HZ. Euhabitability requires: right-size rocky
  planet in HZ + plate tectonics + magnetic field + land masses + oceans + N-O atmosphere.
- **Rare Earth property list** (Taylor 1998; Ward & Brownlee 2000): right-size rocky
  planet in HZ, plate tectonics + magnetic field, land masses + oceans, N-O atmosphere
  with CO₂, large stabilizing moon, Jupiter-analog guardian against impact flux, G-type
  star in galactic habitable zone.
- **Land surfaces as civilization prerequisite:** Simpson (2017) notes that technological
  intelligence (the "fecundity" concept) requires land-based complex life specifically.
  Ocean-only biospheres may sustain microbial life but complex, tool-using civilizations
  appear to require continental surfaces.

**Nutrient Cycling:**
- Phosphorus and nitrogen from continental weathering, transported by rivers, are critical
  nutrients. Seafloor weathering provides the same at much lower rates due to lower flow
  velocity, different pH, and lack of erosion exposing fresh surfaces.
- **Implication:** All-ocean worlds are doubly disadvantaged: less photosynthetic surface
  area AND poorer nutrient availability.

**Limitation:** Paper is a review, not a primary data study. Quantitative probability
estimates for tectonic mode, land-ocean balance, and magnetic field lifetime are cited
from referenced papers (Höning & Spohn, Simpson 2017, etc.), not newly derived here.
Treat probability estimates as literature consensus, not new empirical results.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Tectonic regime probability (approximate, from Solar System analogs + theory):**
```
TectonicRegime        | Probability | Notes
----------------------|-------------|----------------------------------
Stagnant-lid          | ~0.65       | Natural default; Venus/Mars analog
Heat-pipe             | ~0.10       | Very early hot planets; Io analog
Squishy-lid           | ~0.05       | Transitional; early Archean analog
Mobile-lid / Plates   | ~0.20       | Earth-like; contingent on mass + water + cooling
```
*Note: These are rough order-of-magnitude estimates from Solar System analogy and
theoretical arguments. Human verification against Höning & Spohn (2016, 2023) and
Lourenço et al. (2026) is required before using as StarGen defaults.*

**Land-ocean balance probability (Höning & Spohn 2016, 2023):**
```
LandOceanConfig       | Probability | NPP (relative) | O₂ productivity
----------------------|-------------|----------------|------------------
All-ocean (< 10% land)| ~0.35       | ~0.01×         | May not reach 18%
Mostly ocean (10-40%) | ~0.25       | ~0.30×         | Reduced
Balanced (40-70% land)| ~0.20       | ~1.0×          | Earth-like (optimal)
Mostly land (70-90%)  | ~0.15       | ~0.40×         | Reduced (water limited)
All-land (> 90%)      | ~0.05       | ~0.01×         | Water-limited
```
*Warning: "balanced" is the LEAST LIKELY outcome; all-ocean or all-land are more common.*

**StarGen tectonic mode selection:**
```csharp
// Tectonic regime roll (Spohn et al. 2026, Höning & Spohn 2016/2023)
TectonicRegime regime;
double roll = rng.NextDouble();

if (planet.MassEarth < 0.5 || planet.AgeGyr > 8.0)
{
    // Low mass or old planet: stagnant-lid very likely (lost heat budget)
    regime = TectonicRegime.StagnantLid;
}
else if (roll < 0.65)
{
    regime = TectonicRegime.StagnantLid;
}
else if (roll < 0.75)
{
    regime = TectonicRegime.HeatPipe;
}
else if (roll < 0.80)
{
    regime = TectonicRegime.SquishyLid;
}
else
{
    regime = TectonicRegime.PlateTectonics;  // ~0.20 base probability
}
planet.TectonicRegime = regime;
```

**Land-ocean balance selection (Spohn et al. 2026):**
```csharp
// Land fraction distribution (biased away from Earth-like balanced; Höning & Spohn)
double landFraction;
double balanceRoll = rng.NextDouble();

if (balanceRoll < 0.35)
{
    landFraction = rng.NextDouble() * 0.10;              // all-ocean
}
else if (balanceRoll < 0.60)
{
    landFraction = 0.10 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.30;       // mostly ocean
}
else if (balanceRoll < 0.80)
{
    landFraction = 0.40 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.30;       // balanced (Earth-like)
}
else if (balanceRoll < 0.95)
{
    landFraction = 0.70 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.20;       // mostly land
}
else
{
    landFraction = 0.90 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.10;       // all-land
}
planet.LandFraction = landFraction;

// Scale NPP and O2 productivity by land fraction (Spohn et al. 2026)
// All-ocean or all-land → NPP ~1% of Earth's
double nppFactor = 4.0 * landFraction * (1.0 - landFraction);  // peaks at 0.5 land
planet.NPPFactor = Math.Max(0.01, nppFactor);
```

**O₂ productivity dependency on land-ocean balance (Taiz2026 + Balbi2023 interaction):**
```csharp
// On all-ocean worlds, O2 productivity may not reach Balbi's 18% threshold
// On balanced worlds, O2 accumulation follows Earth-analog timeline
double o2ProductivityModifier = Math.Max(0.01, 4.0 * planet.LandFraction *
    (1.0 - planet.LandFraction));
double effectiveO2Rate = baseO2Rate * o2ProductivityModifier;
// If effectiveO2Rate too low, Balbi2023 threshold may not be reached in stellar lifetime
```

**Euhabitable gate (adds to ForganRice criteria):**
```csharp
// Additional "euhabitable" requirements beyond HZ placement (Spohn et al. 2026)
bool euhabitable =
    planet.TectonicRegime == TectonicRegime.PlateTectonics &&
    planet.HasMagneticField &&
    planet.LandFraction >= 0.10 &&              // some land required for complex life
    planet.AtmosphereType == AtmosphereType.Nitrogen_Oxygen &&
    planet.IsInHabitableZone;

planet.IsEuhabitable = euhabitable;
// Civilization probability gated on euhabitable, not just HZ placement
```

**User levers:**
- `TectonicRegimeModel` (enum: SpohnEtAl2026 / EarthCentric / Disabled)
  - SpohnEtAl2026: stagnant-lid as default; mobile-lid ~20% probability
  - EarthCentric: assume plate tectonics as default for HZ rocky worlds
  - Disabled: no tectonic regime logic
- `LandOceanModel` (enum: SpohnEtAl2026 / Uniform / Disabled)
  - SpohnEtAl2026: biased distribution with balanced as least likely
  - Uniform: equal probability across land fractions
  - Disabled: use fixed land fraction (default 0.29)

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Tectonic regime and land-ocean balance as civilization prerequisites
SpohnEtAl2026 establishes that Earth-like plate tectonics and balanced land/ocean coverage
are not the expected outcome for rocky exoplanets — they are contingent states that require
specific conditions of planetary mass, cooling history, and water budget. The "euhabitable"
concept (Glaser et al. 2026, cited here) formalizes what StarGen's ForganRice2010 criteria
only partially address.

**Primary updates to StarGen planet generation:**
1. Add `TectonicRegime` field to planet data model (StagnantLid / HeatPipe / SquishyLid
   / PlateTectonics); sample from probability table based on planet mass and age
2. Add `LandFraction` field; sample from biased distribution (balanced least likely)
3. Add `NPPFactor` derived from land fraction (peaks at 0.5 land/ocean balance)
4. Add `IsEuhabitable` flag gated on tectonic + magnetic + atmospheric + land requirements
5. Gate coal formation (Taiz2026) on plate tectonics AND adequate land fraction
6. Scale O₂ accumulation rate (Balbi2023) by NPPFactor
