## Abstract
The Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence (SETI) has thus far failed to provide a
convincing detection of intelligent life. In the wake of this null signal, many
"contact pessimistic" hypotheses have been formulated, the most famous of which is
the Rare Earth Hypothesis. It postulates that although terrestrial planets may be
common, the exact environmental conditions that Earth enjoys are rare, perhaps unique.
As a result, simple microbial life may be common, but complex metazoans (and hence
intelligence) will be rare. This paper uses Monte Carlo Realisation Techniques to
investigate the Rare Earth Hypothesis, in particular the environmental criteria
considered imperative to the existence of intelligence on Earth. By comparing with a
less restrictive, more optimistic hypothesis, the data indicates that if the Rare Earth
hypothesis is correct, intelligent civilisation will indeed be relatively rare. Studying
the separations of pairs of civilisations shows that most intelligent civilisation pairs
(ICPs) are unconnected: that is, they will not be able to exchange signals at lightspeed
in the limited time that both are extant. However, the few ICPs that are connected are
strongly connected, being able to participate in numerous exchanges of signals. This may
provide encouragement for SETI researchers: although the Rare Earth Hypothesis is in
general a contact-pessimistic hypothesis, it may be a "soft" or "exclusive" hypothesis,
i.e. it may contain facets that are latently contact-optimistic.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- The paper tests the Rare Earth Hypothesis (REH) of Ward & Brownlee (2000) by Monte
  Carlo sampling of 8 "Earth Criteria" required for complex intelligent life
- **8 Earth Criteria used in the MC simulation:**
  1. Planet in the habitable zone (HZ) of its host star
  2. Host star in the Galactic Habitable Zone (GHZ): not too close to the galactic center
     (radiation hazard) nor too far (metal-poor)
  3. Terrestrial planet with mass sufficient to retain an atmosphere (~0.1–5 M_Earth)
  4. Low orbital eccentricity (e < ~0.2) to avoid climate extremes
  5. Presence of a large moon (stabilizes axial tilt, suppresses chaotic obliquity)
  6. Active plate tectonics (carbon cycle, long-term climate regulation)
  7. Oxygen-rich atmosphere (complex metazoan life threshold)
  8. Jupiter-class giant planet in the outer system (shields inner system from impacts)
- **Key result (REH scenario):** Civilizations are rare. The precise fraction depends on
  how each criterion is modeled, but under REH assumptions the number of communicating
  civilizations in the MW is ~O(1)–O(10²) at any time.
- **Key result (optimistic scenario):** Without the restrictive criteria, civilizations
  are much more common; the galaxy can contain O(10⁴)–O(10⁶) civilizations.
- **Soft REH finding:** Even under REH, the few civilization pairs that can exchange
  signals are *strongly connected* — they have enough time overlap to exchange many
  signals. The "exclusive club" interpretation: rare civilizations but high connectivity
  within the club.
- **Civilization lifetime sensitivity:** The model is strongly sensitive to assumed
  civilization lifetime L. Short L (< 10³ yr) → nearly all ICPs unconnected.
  Long L (> 10⁶ yr) → connected fraction rises sharply.
- **Limitation:** The 8 criteria are Earth-centric; the necessity of each is debated
  (e.g., large moon may not be required for axial stability; plate tectonics may not be
  the only carbon cycle mechanism).
- **Limitation:** MC simulation assumes each criterion is statistically independent, which
  is physically unrealistic (e.g., giant planet shield correlates with outer disk dynamics).
- **Limitation:** The paper does not model civilization-planet feedback, resource depletion,
  or the Anthropocene-style transition to a "technosphere" (see FrankEtAl2018).
- The galactic model uses the Lineweaver (2004) GHZ: a ring of habitability peaking at
  ~7–8 kpc from the galactic center, expanding outward over cosmic time.

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**Direct follow-up from same group:**
- Forgan, D. H. (2017). Revising the Revised Drake Equation. International Journal of
  Astrobiology, 18(2), 151–160. arXiv:1609.09744. Extends the MC approach with updated
  occurrence rates and a more complete probabilistic framework.
- ForgnEtAl2017.txt (in corpus) — same lead author, galactic habitable zone CLUES
  simulation. Different paper; that one is about GHZ structure, not civilization probability.

**Rare Earth critique and alternatives:**
- Lineweaver, C. H., & Davis, T. M. (2002) Astrobiology 2, 293 — quantitative GHZ model;
  the spatial prior used in this paper. LineweaverDavis2002 if in corpus.
- Chyba, C. F., & Hand, K. P. (2005) ARA&A 43, 31 — critiques strong REH; argues
  subsurface ocean worlds (Europa, Enceladus) could host life without any of the 8 criteria.
- BalbiAndFrank2023.txt (in corpus) — adds the oxygen atmosphere bottleneck for
  *technospheres* (combustion-capable civilizations) specifically. This refines criterion 7.
- Behroozi, P., & Peeples, M. S. (2015) MNRAS 454, 1811 — Earth formed early in cosmic
  history; most Earth-like planets may be in the future. Affects civilization timing.

**Superseding work on criterion probabilities:**
- TESS/Kepler era occurrence rates (post-2018): occurrence rate updates from
  Dressing & Charbonneau (2015), Bryson et al. (2021) affect criterion 1 probability.
- For the large-moon criterion: Elser et al. (2011) Icarus 214, 357 — Monte Carlo of
  moon-forming impacts; ~5–10% of rocky planets around Sun-like stars form a large moon.
- For giant planet shield: Horner & Jones (2012) MNRAS 425, 749 — giant planets may
  INCREASE impact rate for some configurations ("Jupiter as friend or foe").

**Citation discovery:**
- ADS: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IJAsB...9...73F

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Civilization emergence probability pipeline
ForganRice2010 provides the gate-model foundation for StarGen's civilization emergence
probability. Each of the 8 criteria maps to a testable condition on the generated planet
or system.

**Generation rules:**
```csharp
// CivilizationEmergenceModel enum
public enum CivilizationEmergenceModel
{
    RareEarth,   // 8-gate product; conservative probabilities
    Optimistic   // relaxed probabilities; most gates near 0.3–0.7
}

// Per-planet civilization probability stack
// (each gate is a Bernoulli trial; all must pass for civilization)
double p_civ = 1.0;
p_civ *= p_hz_orbit(planet.SemiMajorAxisAU, star.LuminosityLsun);   // gate 1
p_civ *= p_ghz(star.GalacticRadiusKpc, star.AgeGyr);                // gate 2
p_civ *= p_terrestrial_mass(planet.MassEarths);                      // gate 3
p_civ *= p_low_eccentricity(planet.OrbitalEccentricity);             // gate 4
p_civ *= p_large_moon(system.HasLargeMoonForPlanet(planet));         // gate 5
p_civ *= p_plate_tectonics(planet.MassEarths, planet.CompositionType); // gate 6
p_civ *= p_oxygen_atmosphere(planet, star);                          // gate 7 + BalbiAndFrank
p_civ *= p_giant_shield(system.HasOuterGiant);                       // gate 8
```

**Model family selector:**
`GenerationSettings.CivilizationEmergenceModel` (enum: RareEarth / Optimistic)
- RareEarth: use conservative probability table (column 2 above)
- Optimistic: use relaxed probability table (column 3 above)
- Default: Optimistic (avoids empty galaxies in typical play sessions)

**Civilization lifetime L (user lever):**
`GenerationSettings.CivilizationLifetimeYears` (float, default 1e4)
- Controls how long a civilization remains "detectable" / "active"
- Feeds into population framework `CivilizationEra` annotation
- Short L → isolated civilizations; long L → overlapping / interconnected
