## Abstract
In the coming years and decades, advanced space- and ground-based observatories will
allow an unprecedented opportunity to probe the atmospheres and surfaces of potentially
habitable exoplanets for signatures of life. Preparing for this challenge requires a
thorough understanding of biosignatures and their potential false positives. Here we
provide a comprehensive review of the state of the science of biosignature science as
of 2017–2018, including: gaseous biosignatures (O₂, O₃, CH₄, N₂O, CH₃Cl, and others);
surface biosignatures (vegetation red edge, pigments); temporal biosignatures; and
technosignatures. We discuss abiotic and false-positive sources for each biosignature,
and the importance of context in interpreting combinations of biosignatures. We describe
the observational capabilities needed and expected from JWST, ELTs, and future direct-
imaging missions. This review serves as a community reference for the biosignature
science community.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Primary biosignature gases (ranked by detectability and reliability):**
  1. O₂ (0.55 μm, 0.76 μm A-band) — most robust biosignature; abiotic sources exist
     (photolysis) but are distinguishable with context. pO₂ ≥ 1% required for detection.
  2. O₃ (9.6 μm) — good proxy for O₂; detectable at O₂ fractions as low as 0.1-1%.
     Robust because O₃ is photochemically linked to O₂.
  3. CH₄ (7.7 μm) — biotic CH₄ is detectable; abiotic sources (serpentinization) can
     be false positive. CH₄ + O₂ together are very strong biosignature combination
     (would be in chemical disequilibrium without biology).
  4. N₂O (7.8 μm, 17 μm) — primarily biological; very low abiotic production rate.
     Hard to detect at current concentrations but strong if abundant.
  5. CH₃Cl (9.7 μm) — entirely biological on Earth; low abundance makes detection hard.
- **False positives and context:**
  - O₂ false positive: photolysis of CO₂/H₂O in M-dwarf systems can produce abiotic O₂.
    Detection of CO or lack of H₂O absorption can distinguish.
  - CH₄ false positive: geothermal/volcanic serpentinization. Distinguish by CO₂/H₂ context.
  - The importance of COMBINATIONS: no single biosignature is definitive; combinations
    and context (stellar type, planetary mass, presence of water) reduce false positives.
- **Surface biosignatures:**
  - Vegetation red edge (~0.7 μm): spectral feature of chlorophyll absorption cutoff.
    Detectable in disk-averaged spectra at sufficient vegetation coverage (>20% land area).
  - Carotenoid-dominated worlds (purple/orange surface): detectable if dominant photopigment.
  - Limitation: requires spatial resolution or orbital phase curves; difficult with disk average.
- **Technosignatures (civilization-level):**
  - Industrial pollutants: CFCs, NO₂ (anthropogenic), SF₆ — no natural sources; definitive
    if detected. Detection requires very sensitive future telescopes.
  - Waste heat: thermal infrared excess beyond stellar irradiation. Detectable for Type I+
    Kardashev civilizations.
  - Radio leakage: not atmospheric but radio SETI (covered elsewhere).
  - Nightside illumination: artificial lighting detectable on very nearby worlds.
- **The disequilibrium principle:** Life maintains planetary atmospheres out of thermodynamic
  equilibrium (e.g., Earth: O₂ + CH₄ coexist, which would react in equilibrium). Detecting
  disequilibrium is a general biosignature strategy.
- **JWST capabilities:** Can detect O₃ in optimal systems; CH₄ in transiting atmospheres.
  Full biosignature suite requires future large direct-imaging missions (HWO, LIFE).
- **Limitation:** This review covers 2017 knowledge; JWST results and Ariel mission data
  substantially extend the empirical basis. Treat as the foundational reference to be
  supplemented by post-2018 JWST results.
- **Limitation:** Most biosignature analysis assumes Earth-like biochemistry (water, carbon,
  oxygen). Alternative biochemistry would produce different signatures.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Biosignature flags and worldbuilding descriptors
SchwietermanEtAl2018 provides the scientific basis for how StarGen characterizes
a planet's atmospheric biosignature state — what an observer would detect.

**Biosignature profile generation:**
```csharp
public static BiosignatureProfile GenerateBiosignatureProfile(
    Planet planet, CivilizationState civState, GalaxyRng rng)
{
    var profile = new BiosignatureProfile();

    // O₂/O₃ flag from atmospheric O₂
    profile.OxygenBiosignature = planet.AtmosphereProps.OxygenPartialPressureBar > 0.01;
    profile.DisequilibriumFlag = profile.OxygenBiosignature
                               && planet.AtmosphereProps.MethaneConcentrationPpm > 10;

    // Vegetation red edge
    profile.VegetationRedEdge = planet.VegetationLandFraction > 0.20
                               && rng.NextDouble() < 0.7;

    // Technosignatures
    profile.IndustrialPollutants = civState == CivilizationState.AnthropoceneTransition
                                 || civState == CivilizationState.SustainableSteadyState;
    profile.WasteHeatAnomaly = civState == CivilizationState.SustainableSteadyState
                              && planet.CivilizationTechLevel >= 9;

    // Anomalies for collapsed civilizations
    profile.DecayingAnomaly = civState == CivilizationState.Collapsed;

    return profile;
}
```
