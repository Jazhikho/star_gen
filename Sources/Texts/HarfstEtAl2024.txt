## Abstract
This paper proposes a two-dimensional model of political legitimacy, distinguishing
between internal legitimacy (the belief of citizens that their government has the
right to rule) and external legitimacy (the recognition by other political units that
a government's authority is valid). The authors argue that existing political legitimacy
measurements conflate these two dimensions, leading to confusion in comparative
political analysis. They develop operational measures for both dimensions and apply
them to a comparative dataset. Key findings: internal and external legitimacy are
empirically distinct and can vary independently; a government can have high internal
legitimacy (popular support) while having low external legitimacy (contested
internationally) and vice versa. This two-dimensional model improves prediction of
political stability and regime durability.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Two dimensions of political legitimacy:**
  1. **Internal legitimacy:** Citizens' belief that their government has the right to
     govern. Measured by survey-based support, institutional trust, national identity
     alignment. High internal legitimacy → government can govern without constant
     coercive enforcement.
  2. **External legitimacy:** Recognition by other states/actors that the government
     is the valid authority. Measured by diplomatic recognition, international
     organization membership, treaty participation.
- **Key finding — independence:** The two dimensions can vary independently. Examples:
  - High internal / Low external: non-recognized states (Kosovo, Taiwan at some points,
    de facto governments in conflict zones)
  - Low internal / High external: internationally recognized authoritarian governments
    with suppressed domestic populations
  - High/High: mature democratic states (OAO in Murphy2023.txt terminology)
  - Low/Low: failed states (fragile in Murphy2023 terminology)
- **Stability prediction:** Political stability correlates with BOTH dimensions.
  Either can be a weak point — a government with high external but low internal
  legitimacy is vulnerable to internal revolt; the reverse is vulnerable to external
  intervention.
- **Factions and contestation:** Low internal legitimacy predicts higher faction
  activity — multiple actors competing for the government's claim to rule.
- **Limitation:** Measurement of internal legitimacy in authoritarian contexts is
  difficult; surveys undercount true dissent.

## StarGen applicability
Provides the **two-axis legitimacy model** for StarGen's government characterization.
Adds the internal/external distinction to the government profile:
- Internal legitimacy → faction count / intensity (low internal = more factions)
- External legitimacy → diplomatic status, starport access, trade partner count
- Together with Murphy2023 (OAO/LAO/Fragile) and AuerbachThachil2024 (capacity),
  this completes the four-axis government profile: [capacity, legitimacy_internal,
  legitimacy_external, economic_openness]

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Two-axis legitimacy → StarGen outputs:**
```csharp
// Internal legitimacy → faction count
double internalLegitimacy = SampleInternalLegitimacy(govType, rng);
// 0.0 = no popular support; 1.0 = full popular mandate
int factionCount = (int)Math.Round(rng.NextGaussian(
    3 + (1.0 - internalLegitimacy) * 8,  // low legitimacy → more factions
    1.5));
factionCount = Math.Clamp(factionCount, 0, 12);

// External legitimacy → diplomatic openness
double externalLegitimacy = SampleExternalLegitimacy(govType, oaoCategory, rng);
// High external → trade access, starport class
bool hasTradeAccess = externalLegitimacy > 0.50;
int starportClassBonus = (int)(externalLegitimacy * 3); // 0-3 bonus

// Stability flag
bool politicallyStable = internalLegitimacy > 0.40 && externalLegitimacy > 0.40;
```

