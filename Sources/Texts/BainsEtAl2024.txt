## Abstract
Life is a complex, dynamic chemical system that requires a dense fluid solvent in which to take place. A common assumption is that the most likely solvent for life is liquid water, and some researchers argue that water is the only plausible solvent. However, a persistent theme in astrobiological research postulates that other liquids might be cosmically common, and could be solvents for the chemistry of life. In this paper we present a new framework for the analysis of candidate solvents for life, and deploy this framework to review substances that have been suggested as solvent candidates. We categorize each solvent candidate through four criteria: occurrence, solvation, solute stability and solvent chemical functionality. Our semi-quantitative approach addresses all the requirements for a solvent not only from the point of view of its chemical properties but also from the standpoint of their biochemical function. Only the protonating solvents fulfil all the chemical requirements to be a solvent for life, and of those only water and concentrated sulfuric acid are also likely to be abundant in a rocky planetary context. Among the non-protonating solvents liquid CO2 stands out as a planetary solvent, and its potential as a solvent for life should be explored. We conclude with a discussion of whether it is possible for a biochemistry to change solvents, as an adaptation to radical changes in a planet’s environment. Our analysis provides the basis for prioritizing future experimental work exploring potential complex chemistry on other planets.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
*Human verification required before using semi-quantitative criterion scores as hard StarGen filters. Table 1 in the PDF uses symbolic ticks/crosses; transcribe from the PDF before encoding as machine rules. Origin-of-life (OOL) criteria are explicitly excluded—claims apply to maintenance biochemistry, not abiogenesis pathways.*

- **Framework:** Any plausible bulk solvent for life must be scored against **four criteria**: (1) **Occurrence**—stable abiotic reservoirs on or near rocky surfaces (clouds/crust) over geological time, dense enough and low enough viscosity for macromolecular motion; (2) **Solvation**—dissolves a wide range of solutes including ions and polymers yet leaves other classes insoluble (enabling barriers, amphipaths, phase separation); (3) **Solute stability**—chemically diverse metabolites survive over biologically relevant timescales at temperatures where the solvent is liquid; (4) **Solvent chemical functionality**—the solvent can participate in metabolism (protonation, hydrolysis, etc.), not only act as passive medium. Criterion 4 is acknowledged as **Earth-life-prejudiced** and treated as **weaker** than the first three.
- **Occurrence scope:** Analysis emphasizes **surface** (or near-surface / cloud) lakes/oceans pragmatically linked to detectability; subsurface-only life is noted as hard to observe on exoplanets. **Self-generated solvent only** (no abiotic reservoir) is argued to make ecosystems fragile to stress—strong preference for environmentally supplied solvent.
- **Main survey conclusion (Section 3.12, Table 1):** **Liquid water** meets all four criteria and is judged the **strongest** overall candidate. **Concentrated sulfuric acid (roughly >90% w/w)** also meets all four (with **solute stability** flagged uncertain/qualified in Table 1 as (√)), and is argued **plausibly abundant** on rocky planets in **water-depleted** scenarios (Venus-like; also speculated for **post-migration M-dwarf** “belatedly habitable” worlds after primordial baking). **Liquid CO₂** scores well on occurrence and solute stability, **partially** on solvation, and **fails** chemical-functionality-as-proton donor in their framing; it is singled out for **future work**. **Cryogenic solvents** (CH₄, C₂H₆, N₂, etc.) are judged **very poor**—common but fail **solvation** and **functionality** (and cite near-zero polymer solubility). **Liquid ammonia** as **anhydrous bulk solvent** fails **Occurrence** (photochemical/thermal lability, miscibility with water driving alkaline-aqueous chemistry; Europa-style oceans **>~80% water** in their discussion; “ammonia Europa” ammonia loss scenario).
- **Protonating vs non-protonating:** Only **protonating** solvents with strong hydrogen-bond networks satisfy **all chemical requirements** in their analysis; among those, **water** and **concentrated H₂SO₄** are the pair argued both **chemically adequate** and **planetarily plausible**. **NH₃, HF** and several others fail occurrence or stability gates despite favorable solvation in some cases.
- **Mixtures and solvent replacement (Sections 4–5):** Most mixtures are dominated by one component’s chemistry; exceptions discussed include **freezing-point depression** (e.g. water–ammonia down to **176 K** cited; H₂O₂–H₂O; H₂SO₄–H₂O lowering freezing of concentrated acid). **Sulfuric acid** shows **concentration-dependent solvent identity** (~**80% vs 95% w/w** crossover toward “water-like” vs “distinct acid solvent”; **70–100% w/w** regime has non-linear chemistry). **Solvent replacement** over geological time (e.g. Venus water → acid clouds; red-dwarf planets to **liquid CO₂** oceans) is argued **conceivable** with **limited terrestrial precedent** (yeast ethanol tolerance **~25%**, glycerol, D₂O)—**not** demonstrated for wholesale solvent swaps.
- **Liquid rock** rejected for solute stability (thermal destruction of complexity). **Supercritical CO₂** treated separately from liquid CO₂ for occurrence/solvation. **Ionic liquids**—chemically versatile but fail natural occurrence.
- **Viscosity:** Pitch excluded; Branscomb & Russell “overdamped” argument noted with counterpoints; SI ties viscosities of life’s cytoplasmic milieu to limits; **liquid CO₂** viscosity lies **below** measured Earth-life water-based milieus—authors flag unknown whether that is an absolute barrier.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications
*Values below are taken from the paper text, Table 1 summary, and SI excerpts in the PDF. Where the authors give ranges or qualitative ticks, StarGen should expose **assumption levers**, not single hard caps.*

**Criterion 1 — Occurrence (planetary mechanics).** Requires **geologically stable**, **abiotically sourced**, **dense**, **low-to-moderate viscosity** fluid on **rocky** surfaces or **cloud layers** (pressure roughly **0–1000 bar** stated as “little effect” except **supercritical CO₂**). **Mercury** as bulk solvent excluded by **~2 ppb** crustal abundance example. **Photochemical/thermal instability** excludes or down-ranks **NH₃** (N₂ sink), **H₂S**, **SO₂** (context-dependent), **H₂O₂**, **formamide**, **hydrazine**, **methanol** accumulation, **HCN** oceans (runaway polymerization risk), **F₂O**, etc., each with narrative thresholds in Section 3. **M-dwarf “baked then migrated”** scenario for **H₂SO₄** accumulation tied to **pre-main-sequence ~1000×** luminosity spike vs main-sequence (Hayashi & Nakano–class argument cited). **Titan** surface temperature range cited **90–94 K** (**~4 K** span) vs Earth **220–320 K** (**100 K**) for intuition about **narrow liquid windows** on cold worlds.

**Criterion 2 — Solvation (macromolecular / mesoscale mechanics).** **Hydrogen-bond network** and **solvophobic** (or analog) effects favor **water, NH₃, HF, conc. H₂SO₄** for amphipathic assembly; **liquid CO₂** allows **some** amphiphile behavior without permanent dipole (cite detergent-like effects). **Metal ions** require solvation shells; **polar / self-ionizing** solvents favored. **Cryogens:** cite **no** known **polymer systems soluble** in liquid methane (Petkowski et al. 2020 cited in cryogen section); solubility of large organics generally poor—**mechanics implication:** procedural “Titan lake biochemistry” should default to **low confidence** unless user mode overrides.

**Criterion 3 — Solute stability (temperature–chemistry coupling).** **Terrestrial organics** largely unstable in **water above ~300 °C** (supercritical hydrolysis context). **Liquid rock** rejected (**>~thousands K**). **Concentrated H₂SO₄:** sugars unstable classically, but authors argue **other organics** can be stable (cite Seager et al., Spacek et al., Petkowski et al., Seager et al. 2024 series)—**stability vs concentration** is **non-monotonic** (SI rate plots: same compound can speed up or slow down with % acid). **DNA hydrolysis** in bone cited **~5.5×10⁻⁶** bases/year at preservation temperatures; **carbamoyl phosphate** half-life **~0.5–1.5 s** at **122 °C** vs **NADH** **<10 min**—illustrates **required spread** of degradation timescales any solvent must support.

**Criterion 4 — Solvent chemical functionality (weakest gate).** Water participates in **hydrolysis**, **proton gradients**, **hydration of charges**. Authors allow that **carrier-based** metabolism could reduce reliance on solvent reactivity but deem **protonating** solvents more plausible **overall**. **Cryogens** are **chemically inert** as bulk media—fail functionality in practice.

### Per-solvent temperature / pressure gates

**LiquidWater**
- T_min: 273 K (pure); down to ~176 K with ammonia eutectic (not default)
- T_max: ~573 K at sufficient pressure (hydrothermal; above this hydrolysis destroys organics)
- P: any; liquid window wide
- StarGen rule: eligible whenever surface T 273–373 K and liquid surface water present

**ConcentratedSulfuricAcid**
- Concentration band: 81–100% w/w (outside this band, chemistry reverts toward aqueous)
- T_min: 241 K (at 94% w/w freezing point); 283 K for pure acid
- T_max: ~570 K (decomposition upper bound)
- P: modest pressure range; cloud-layer default (~0.5–2 bar in Venus analogue context)
- Requires: global desiccation (water depleted); SO2 photochemistry source; rocky surface or cloud layer
- StarGen rule: eligible on Venus-analogue worlds (high albedo, slow rotation, desiccated) or post-baked M-dwarf planets where H2O was stripped during pre-MS luminosity spike (~1000× excess). Requires planet_type == Rocky and water_inventory < threshold.

**LiquidCO2**
- Requires: P > CO2 critical pressure (~7.38 MPa ≈ 73 bar) AND T < critical T (304 K) for liquid phase; below this CO2 sublimes at Earth-like pressures
- Density threshold for macromolecular solvation: ~800 kg/m³ (flag if below; solvation uncertain)
- StarGen rule: eligible only on high-pressure cold worlds (thick CO2 atmosphere or subsurface). Default plausibility = LOW (biochemistry template not specified). Expose as SpaceOpera/Speculative lever only unless user explicitly enables CO2 biochemistry module.

**CryogenicHydrocarbon** (CH4, C2H6, N2)
- T range: Titan surface ~90–94 K (~4 K liquid window — very narrow)
- Liquid window is far narrower than Earth's (~100 K span); cite as fragility indicator
- StarGen rule: eligible only on outer-system bodies (T_surface < 100 K, N2/CH4 atmosphere). Default plausibility = VERY LOW. Polymer solubility effectively zero under this paper's review — flag habitability confidence as speculative unless user overrides.

### Decision rules (pseudo-code sketch)

```
function EvaluateSolventPlausibility(planet, mode):
    candidates = GetCandidateSolvents(planet)  // from T/P/composition
    for each solvent in candidates:
        scores = GetCriterionScores(solvent)    // from matrix above
        if mode == HardSF and any(scores[0..2] == Fail):
            eliminate(solvent)
            continue
        weight = GenerationModeWeight[mode][solvent]
        overall = weight * product(scores)
        planet.SolventPlausibility[solvent] = overall
    return highest-scoring remaining solvent (or None)

function GetCandidateSolvents(planet):
    candidates = []
    if planet.SurfaceTemperature in [273, 373] and planet.HasLiquidWater:
        candidates.add(LiquidWater)
    if planet.Type == Rocky and planet.WaterInventory < DesiccationThreshold
       and planet.SurfaceTemperature in [241, 570]:
        candidates.add(ConcentratedSulfuricAcid)
    if planet.AtmospherePressure > 73_bar and planet.SurfaceTemperature < 304:
        candidates.add(LiquidCO2)
    if planet.SurfaceTemperature in [88, 95] and planet.HasHydrocarbonLakes:
        candidates.add(CryogenicHydrocarbon)
    return candidates
```
