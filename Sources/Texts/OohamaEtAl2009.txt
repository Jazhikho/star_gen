## Abstract
This paper presents bulge-disk decompositions for 737 spiral (Sa–Sd) and lenticular
(S0) galaxies drawn from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. Using a growth curve fitting
method applied to SDSS g-band and r-band photometry, the authors decompose each galaxy
into a de Vaucouleurs bulge component and an exponential disk component, measuring
effective radius (r_e), effective surface brightness (μ_e), total magnitudes, and
bulge-to-total (B/T) luminosity ratios. The main results are: (1) bulge properties
change systematically along the Hubble sequence (S0→Sc), with earlier types having
larger, brighter, more luminous bulges; (2) disk properties are nearly universal and
do not vary significantly from S0 to Sc types, suggesting that disk scale lengths and
surface brightnesses are relatively independent of bulge prominence; (3) B/T ratios
decrease from early (S0: ~0.4–0.6) to late types (Sc: ~0.1–0.2).

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- Bulge effective radius: increases slightly from Sc to S0 (bulge grows with earlier type)
- Bulge effective surface brightness: brighter in earlier types (denser central regions)
- Bulge absolute magnitude: more luminous in earlier types
- Disk properties: scale length h_disk and disk central surface brightness μ_0 are
  NEARLY UNIVERSAL across S0–Sc; variation is ~factor 2 and is not a systematic
  trend with Hubble type
- B/T ratio: S0 ≈ 0.4–0.6; Sa ≈ 0.3–0.4; Sb ≈ 0.2–0.3; Sc ≈ 0.1–0.2; Sd ≈ 0.05–0.15
  (approximate ranges from figure reading; exact values require PDF verification)
- Methodology: growth curve fitting is more robust to noise than 2D profile fitting
  for average SDSS image quality; however less flexible (assumes pure de Vaucouleurs
  bulge + exponential disk, no bar or lens component)
- Limitation: no bar component in the decomposition; S0s and early spirals with bars
  may have inflated B/T (bar flux absorbed into bulge component)
- Limitation: only g and r bands; no NIR; colors may not give purely stellar mass
- Limitation: sample limited to SDSS main galaxy sample (bright end); low surface
  brightness galaxies excluded
- Limitation: de Vaucouleurs profile (Sérsic n=4) assumed for all bulges; pseudo-bulges
  (with n<2) are misfit and will have over-estimated effective radii

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**B/T ratio by Hubble type (from paper, approximate):**
- S0 / Lenticular:  B/T ~ 0.45 (range 0.30–0.65)
- Sa:               B/T ~ 0.35 (range 0.20–0.50)
- Sb:               B/T ~ 0.25 (range 0.15–0.40)
- Sc:               B/T ~ 0.15 (range 0.05–0.30)
- Sd / Irregular:   B/T ~ 0.07 (range 0.01–0.20)
These are the primary mappings from Hubble subtype → bulge prominence in StarGen.

**Bulge effective radius (approximate, for L* galaxies):**
- S0: r_e(bulge) ~ 1–3 kpc (depends heavily on galaxy total luminosity)
- Sc: r_e(bulge) ~ 0.3–1 kpc
Bulge radius scales with galaxy stellar mass; Kormendy (1977) relation applies.

**Disk scale length (approximately universal):**
- h_disk ~ 2–5 kpc for L* disk galaxies, regardless of Hubble type
- Variation is primarily driven by total galaxy stellar mass, not Hubble type
- StarGen: for `disk_scale_length_pc`, use a mass-based relation, not a Hubble-type
  dependent one (because disk universality is the key finding)

**Disk central surface brightness (Freeman's law approximation):**
- μ_0 (disk) ≈ 21.65 ± 0.30 mag/arcsec² in B-band (Freeman 1970) — not strongly
  dependent on Hubble type (consistent with disk universality finding)

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**More comprehensive decompositions (important follow-up):**
- Meert, A., Vikram, V., & Bernardi, M. (2015) MNRAS 446, 3943 — "A catalog of
  bulge+disk decompositions and updated photometry for 1.12 million galaxies in SDSS."
  arXiv:1407.6188. Dramatically larger sample; multi-component (bar, ring, lens)
  decompositions. Use as a primary reference if detailed structural statistics are
  needed.
- Gadotti, D. A. (2009) MNRAS 393, 1531 — pseudo-bulge vs classical bulge decompositions
  in SDSS; important for getting Sérsic index distributions. arXiv:0810.1953.
- Simard, L., et al. (2011) ApJS 196, 11 — large SDSS bulge+disk catalog with Sérsic
  fitting. arXiv:1107.1518. Complementary to Meert et al.

**NIR-based decompositions (more accurate for stellar mass):**
- LaurikainenEtAl2010.txt (in corpus) — NIRS0S Ks-band decompositions for S0s and
  spirals; NIR better traces stellar mass, less affected by dust/recent SF.
- Salo, H., et al. (2015) ApJS 219, 4 (S4G survey) — 2352 galaxies at 3.6 μm with
  full multi-component decompositions. arXiv:1503.07711. Gold standard for nearby galaxies.

This paper (with LaurikainenEtAl2010) provides the structural parameter lookup table


**Disk universality rule:**
Do NOT vary disk scale length with Hubble subtype directly.
Instead, scale disk_scale_length_pc from stellar mass:
  h_disk ~ 3.5 kpc × (M_star / 5e10 M_sun)^0.35 (approximate; tunable)
Apply a log-normal scatter of σ ~ 0.25 dex around this mass-based median.

**Bulge radius from B/T:**
  r_e(bulge) ~ r_half_light × sqrt(B/T) × 0.7  (approximate geometric relation)
where r_half_light is derived from total galaxy radius.

properties; `LaurikainenNIRS0S` for NIR-based values preferred for stellar mass accuracy).
