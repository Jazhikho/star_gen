## Abstract
Most of our knowledge about rocky exoplanets is based on their measured mass and radius.
These two parameters are routinely measured and used to categorise different populations of
observed exoplanets. They are also tightly linked to the planet's interior properties. As
such they offer the unique opportunity to interpret observations and potentially infer the
planet's chemistry and structure. Required for the interpretation are models of planetary
interiors, calculated a priori, constrained using other available data, and based on the
physicochemical properties of mineralogical phases. This article offers an overview of the
current knowledge about exoplanet interiors, the fundamental aspects and tools for interior
modelling, and how to improve the constraints on the models, along with a discussion of the
sources of uncertainty. The origin and fate of volatiles, and their role in planetary
evolution, is discussed. The chemistry and structure of planetary interiors have a pivotal
role in the thermal evolution of planets and the development of large-scale properties that
might become observables with future space missions and ground-based surveys.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Mass + radius alone cannot uniquely constrain interior composition (mass-radius
  degeneracy):** A given (mass, radius) pair is consistent with multiple interior
  compositions (e.g., a pure rock world vs. a rocky world with a water layer vs. a rocky
  world with a thin gas envelope can produce the same apparent mass and radius). Any
  single composition assignment from mass/radius alone is an under-constrained inference.
- **Interior modeling tools:** Models use equations of state (EOS) for mineralogical
  phases (iron core, silicate mantle, water/ice layer, gas envelope) to compute density
  profiles that match observed mass and radius. Different EOS choices introduce systematic
  uncertainty.
- **Additional constraints:** Stellar composition (metallicity, Fe/Si ratio), atmospheric
  spectroscopy, transit timing variations (TTVs), and thermal emission can help break the
  degeneracy. Without these, interior models should report probability distributions, not
  single values.
- **Volatiles are key:** The presence and amount of volatiles (H₂O, CO₂, H₂, CH₄)
  dramatically affect the mass-radius relationship. A 5% water mass fraction shifts the
  radius significantly upward relative to a pure-rock world of the same mass. Volatiles
  also drive atmospheric evolution and long-term habitability.
- **Thermal evolution affects observables:** Interior cooling determines whether a planet
  maintains an active dynamo (magnetic field), drives plate tectonics, and sustains
  volcanic outgassing. These are all relevant to habitability and biosignature signatures.
- **Future missions:** Future space missions (PLATO, HWO, LIFE) will provide improved
  mass-radius measurements for many targets, partially breaking the degeneracy via
  improved precision and complementary spectroscopic data.
- **Limitation:** Interior models are calibrated primarily on Solar System data and Earth
  specifically. EOS extrapolations to high pressure (super-Earth interiors) have
  substantial uncertainty.
- **Limitation:** The paper is a review, not primary research. Specific quantitative
  thresholds and model parameters must be verified against the cited primary literature
  (LuquePalle2022, Otegi2020, ChenKipping2017 in StarGen corpus).

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Composition as probability distribution
Baumeister & Miozzi (2025) establishes that composition inferred from mass+radius alone
is fundamentally uncertain. StarGen should expose this uncertainty rather than presenting
false precision in single-composition labels.

**Primary updates:**
1. Replace single `CompositionType` enum field with `CompositionDistribution` object
   that samples from probability-weighted composition classes
2. Show uncertainty in planet inspector UI ("Likely rocky (65%), possibly water-world
   (25%)")
3. Use formation location (SchonbachlerEtAl2025 NC/CC boundary) as a prior to sharpen
   the composition distribution
4. For M4+ host planets (GillisEtAl2026): shift prior toward water-world
