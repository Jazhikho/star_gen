## Abstract
Research in the social sciences often describes social complexity through a combination
of structure, organization, and behavior within human social systems. In this paper,
I argue that these aspects, while important, are conceptually distinct. Specifically,
I distinguish between structural complexity — the organizational properties of a
system — and dynamic complexity — the patterns of behavior and interaction within the
system. I argue that the conflation of these two dimensions leads to conceptual confusion
in the social complexity literature and hampers empirical progress. I propose a framework
for separating and measuring them, and discuss implications for understanding the
"problem-solving" capacity of human social systems. A system can be highly structurally
complex (many differentiated roles, institutions, levels of hierarchy) while being
dynamically simple (highly predictable, routine behavior). Conversely, a system can
be dynamically complex (unpredictable, adaptive behavior) while being structurally
simple (few formal institutions).

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Structural complexity vs. dynamic complexity:**
  - **Structural complexity:** Measures the organizational differentiation of a system
    — number of distinct roles, institutions, hierarchical levels, degree of specialization.
    Maps to: government bureaucracy depth, economic sector differentiation, number of
    distinct social classes.
  - **Dynamic complexity:** Measures the unpredictability and adaptability of system
    behavior — how often the system responds in novel ways to perturbations. Maps to:
    political volatility, economic innovation rate, social change rate.
- **Independence of the two dimensions:** High structural complexity does not imply
  high dynamic complexity. A highly bureaucratic dictatorship can be structurally very
  complex (many ministries, layers of hierarchy) while being dynamically very simple
  (predictable, rigid response to perturbations). An entrepreneurial startup ecosystem
  can be dynamically complex but structurally simple.
- **Problem-solving capacity:** The paper argues that effective social problem-solving
  requires a match between structural and dynamic complexity: complex problems (novel
  crises, technological disruption) require dynamic complexity; routine problems require
  structural complexity. Mismatches → inefficient governance.
- **Implication for civilization modeling:** A civilization's "resilience" is not
  determined by TechLevel or population alone, but by the match between structural
  and dynamic complexity relative to its environmental challenges.
- **Limitation:** The framework is conceptual; operationalization is discussed but
  specific measurement tools are preliminary.
- **Limitation:** The study focuses on contemporary human societies; historical
  and alien applications require extension.

## StarGen applicability
Provides the **structural vs. dynamic complexity distinction** for StarGen's
civilization characterization. This is primarily a conceptual contribution that
informs the descriptive output for a world's governance style:
- High structural complexity + low dynamic complexity → "rigid bureaucracy"
  (stable but slow to adapt; high LawLevel, authoritarian or civil service government)
- High dynamic complexity + low structural complexity → "entrepreneurial chaos"
  (innovative but unstable; lower LawLevel, participatory or corporate government)
- High in both → "mature complex democracy" (OAO states, TL 9+)
- Low in both → "primitive undifferentiated" (pre-state or post-collapse)

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Two-axis complexity profile:**
```csharp
public struct ComplexityProfile
{
    public double Structural;  // 0-1: organizational differentiation
    public double Dynamic;     // 0-1: behavioral adaptability/unpredictability
}

// Structural complexity correlates with:
//   - GovernmentType hierarchy depth
//   - TechLevel (more institutions needed for high tech)
//   - LawLevel (high law = high structural complexity in enforcement)
double structural = (techLevel / 15.0) * 0.6
                  + (lawLevel / 9.0) * 0.3
                  + rng.NextGaussian(0.0, 0.1);

// Dynamic complexity correlates with:
//   - Economic openness (OAO → more adaptive)
//   - InternalLegitimacy (high → more democratic → more adaptive)
//   - Inversely with LawLevel (high enforcement → less adaptive)
double dynamic = oaoScore * 0.4
               + internalLegitimacy * 0.4
               - (lawLevel / 9.0) * 0.2
               + rng.NextGaussian(0.0, 0.1);
```

**Resilience score (for FrankEtAl2018 Anthropocene transition):**
```csharp
// Resilience to the Anthropocene transition depends on problem-solving capacity
// Best: high structural AND dynamic complexity (can organize AND adapt)
// Worst: high structural + low dynamic (organized but rigid → slow to adapt)
double resilience = structural * dynamic;  // geometric mean favors balance
// Used in FrankEtAl2018 adaptabilityRate parameter
double frankAdaptabilityRate = 0.3 + resilience * 0.7;
```
