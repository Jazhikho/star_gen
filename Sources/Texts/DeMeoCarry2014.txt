Source note for StarGen research review

Citation:
DeMeo, F. E., & Carry, B. (2014).
Solar System evolution from compositional mapping of the asteroid belt.
Nature, 505, 629-634.
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3846
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12908

What was reviewed:
- Abstract and source metadata on 2026-04-03.

Key implementation notes used in StarGen:
- The paper maps asteroid-belt composition by heliocentric distance and shows that the belt is not compositionally uniform.
- Broadly, inner regions are more dominated by rocky and differentiated material, while outer regions retain more primitive and volatile-rich bodies.
- For StarGen, the important constraint is that small-body composition and belt placement should respond to system structure, especially frost-line and outer-reservoir context, rather than being assigned independently per belt.

How StarGen uses this:
- As the basis for shifting outer belts and cold small-body reservoirs toward icier and more primitive compositions when outer-system bias is stronger.
- As support for making asteroid/comet placement a system-level consequence of planetary-state scalars rather than a cosmetic label on otherwise identical belts.
