## Abstract
Knez proposes a dynamic macroeconomic model of technology diffusion that accounts for
uneven relative costs of technology implementation, especially wage-level differences.
Using CHAT and Penn World Table data, the paper calibrates country- and
technology-specific adoption curves and explains about half of the variability in
technology density. The resulting curves generalize logistic adoption patterns and vary
with development and wage levels.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Technology density:** Adoption is modeled as relative technology density rather
  than a binary "has/does not have" state.
- **Relative implementation cost:** The model generalizes distance in diffusion models
  into a space of relative technology-implementation costs.
- **Wage/development link:** Wage levels are used as a proxy for country-specific
  relative adoption costs and broader development constraints.
- **Uneven adoption curves:** Adoption curves vary across countries and technologies,
  rather than following one universal logistic curve.
- **Empirical fit:** The calibrated model explains roughly 50% of technology-density
  variability in the selected data.
- **Limitation:** Wage-level logic is human economic history. StarGen should not treat
  it as a universal alien-civilization rule without human review.

## StarGen applicability
Useful for StarGen's technology-diffusion and colony/civilization layers. It supports
separating access to a technology from adoption density, and it suggests that adoption
depends on local cost structure, development level, and existing capability base.

Provides:
- A source anchor for `TechnologyAdoptionCapacity`
- Support for partial adoption rather than all-or-nothing tech levels
- A reason to model imported technology separately from local saturation
- A caution that low-development worlds may adopt some technologies slowly even when
  connected to high-tech trade networks

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Technology density curve:**
```
technology_density(t) = diffusion_curve(local_cost, development, external_access, time)
```

**Generator rule implication:**
- `HighestTechLevel` should not imply full adoption of every lower or contemporary
  technology.
- Colonies may have high imported tech but low local adoption density.
- Trade connectivity should help adoption, but local capacity and cost should still
  constrain saturation.

tech level, trade connectivity, cultural accumulation, state capacity, and surplus. That
