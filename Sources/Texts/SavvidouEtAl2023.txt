## Abstract
Savvidou and Bitsch simulate giant-planet formation through pebble and gas accretion
with migration in viscously evolving protoplanetary disks. They find that giant-planet
formation depends on combinations of disk mass, disk size, early embryo formation,
dust-to-gas ratio, viscosity, fragmentation velocity, and embryo starting location. No
single parameter is sufficient; the outcome comes from interacting disk conditions.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **High disk mass is important:** Giant planets require enough solid material to build
  massive cores before the pebble flux decays.
- **Early formation matters:** Core growth must start early; the article reports that
  starting before about 0.9 Myr is needed in the modeled favorable cases.
- **Disk size matters:** Large disks can maintain long-lasting pebble flux, aiding core
  growth.
- **Dust-to-gas ratio matters:** A higher dust-to-gas ratio can compensate for lower
  disk mass in some cases, but does not remove the need for early formation.
- **Viscosity tradeoff:** Higher viscosity can increase gas accretion and massive-core
  growth, but also increases migration.
- **Embryo location matters:** The highest giant-formation fractions originate roughly
  between 5 and 25 AU in the explored grid.
- **Limitation:** This is a numerical parameter study, not a direct observed occurrence
  distribution. StarGen should use it to shape model structure, not as a literal
  occurrence table.

`PlanetarySystemState` already combines solid budget, gas budget, disk lifetime,
metallicity, migration strength, and snow-line weighting into `GasGiantWeight` and
`SnowLineGiantFormationScalar`.

giant-planet formation depends on multiple interacting parameters. It is not yet close

time, dust-to-gas ratio, fragmentation velocity, and starting-location distributions.
