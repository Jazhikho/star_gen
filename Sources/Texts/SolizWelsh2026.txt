## Abstract
The rise of oxygen in the Earth's atmosphere during the "Great Oxidation Event" (GOE)
is fairly well understood to have occurred ~2.3 billion years ago. There is considerably
greater uncertainty in the date for the origin of oxygenic photosynthesis, but most
studies suggest it occurred significantly earlier, perhaps ~700 million years earlier.
Assuming this time lag is proportional to the rate of oxygen generation, we can estimate
how long it would take for a GOE-like event to occur on a hypothetical Earth-analog planet
orbiting the star TRAPPIST-1 (an ultra-cool M star with Teff ~2560 K). Despite being in
the habitable zone, a hypothetical Earth located in TRAPPIST-1e's orbit would receive only
~0.9% of the "Photosynthetically Active Radiation" (PAR) that the Earth receives from the
Sun. The resulting dramatically slower rate of oxygen generation, combined with the longer
stellar evolution timescale of M dwarfs, suggests that complex life would not have time
to develop before the star exits the main sequence — or at best, complex life would be
severely delayed. We conclude that the dearth of PAR around late M-type stars represents
a fundamental barrier to the development of complex life on exoplanets in their habitable
zones.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **PAR bottleneck for complex life:** TRAPPIST-1 (M8V, Teff ~2560 K) provides only
  ~0.9% of Earth's PAR in the HZ. PAR is the fraction of solar radiation usable by
  photosynthesis (400–700 nm). M dwarf stars emit most of their flux in the near-IR,
  outside the PAR window.
- **Extended GOE timeline:** On Earth, oxygenic photosynthesis started ~2.7 Gya but
  pO₂ only reached the GOE threshold (~1% O₂) at ~2.3 Gya — a ~400 Myr lag due to
  oxygen sink depletion. The paper scales this lag inversely with PAR flux:
  - At 0.9% PAR: oxygen accumulation ~111× slower → GOE delayed by ~44 Gyr
  - This exceeds the expected main-sequence lifetime of TRAPPIST-1 (~10 Gyr remaining)
  - **Conclusion: complex life cannot evolve on TRAPPIST-1e by any conventional route.**
- **General M-dwarf result:** The PAR bottleneck applies broadly to late M-dwarfs
  (M5 and later). Only early M-dwarfs (M0-M3) provide sufficient PAR for a
  timely GOE — but these also have stronger XUV (LugerBarnes2015.txt risk).
- **Stellar type sweet spot for complex life:**
  - K dwarf and G dwarf: ample PAR, manageable XUV → most favorable
  - Early M-dwarf (M0-M3): marginal PAR, higher XUV → possible but risky
  - Late M-dwarf (M4-M9): insufficient PAR → complex life severely inhibited
- **Not "no life" but "no complex life":** The paper argues that microbial life could
  still exist around TRAPPIST-1e; it is specifically the accumulation of oxygen to
  GOE levels (and beyond to Balbi2023's 18% threshold) that is blocked.
- **Limitation:** The oxygen accumulation model is simplified (linear scaling with PAR).
  Real oxygen accumulation is non-linear and depends on multiple geochemical factors.
- **Limitation:** Does not account for alternative photosynthesis using NIR wavelengths
  (some bacteria can use wavelengths up to ~1100 nm — "anoxygenic" photosynthesis, which
  does NOT produce oxygen). NIR photosynthesis could support a biosphere without GOE.
- **Limitation:** TRAPPIST-1 is an extreme case; M3-M4 dwarfs may be borderline.

**GOE delay factor (linear scaling assumption from Soliz & Welsh):**
```
GOE_delay_factor = Earth_GOE_time / PAR_fraction
// At PAR = 0.009 (TRAPPIST-1): delay factor = 1/0.009 = 111×
// Earth GOE time from first oxygenic photosynthesis: ~700 Myr
// Delayed GOE time: 700 Myr × 111 = ~77.7 Gyr (not feasible)
```

**PAR gate implementation for M-dwarf complex life:**
```csharp
// PAR fraction threshold for timely GOE within stellar MS lifetime
// Stellar MS lifetime for M dwarfs: ~10-50+ Gyr (inverse of mass)
// GOE needs to occur within MS lifetime:
// Required PAR_fraction > Earth_GOE_time / (0.75 × stellar_MS_lifetime)
double goldilocksGOE = 0.7e9 / (0.75 * star.MainSequenceLifetimeYears);
bool parGatePassed = star.PARFraction > goldilocksGOE;
// For TRAPPIST-1: goldilocksGOE ~ 0.7e9/(0.75*10e9) = 0.093; PARfraction = 0.009 < 0.093 → FAIL

// Simplified categorical gate:
bool complexLifePossible = star.SpectralType switch
{
    SpectralType.O or SpectralType.B => false,  // too short-lived
    SpectralType.A or SpectralType.F => true,   // ample PAR, short XUV phase
    SpectralType.G or SpectralType.K => true,   // optimal
    SpectralType.M when star.TemperatureK >= 3600 => true,   // M0-M2: marginal OK
    SpectralType.M when star.TemperatureK >= 3200 => false,  // M3-M4: borderline → usually FAIL
    SpectralType.M => false,                    // M5+: PAR insufficient
    _ => true
};
```

**Interaction with Balbi2023 (oxygen bottleneck):**
SolizWelsh establishes that the GOE itself may never happen around late M-dwarfs.
This is a stronger constraint than Balbi's 18% threshold — you can't reach 18% if
you can't even reach the GOE (~1%).

**Updated ForganRice2010 criterion 7 (oxygen atmosphere):**
For M-dwarf host stars (M4+), p_oxygen_atmosphere should be reduced to near-zero:
```csharp
// Modify ForganRice criterion 7 for stellar type
double p_oxygen = star.SpectralType switch
{
    SpectralType.G or SpectralType.K => defaultOxygenProbability,
    SpectralType.M when star.TemperatureK >= 3600 => defaultOxygenProbability * 0.5,
    SpectralType.M => defaultOxygenProbability * 0.02,  // PAR severely limits O₂
    _ => defaultOxygenProbability
};
```

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**Related M-dwarf habitability papers in corpus:**
- LugerBarnes2015.txt — M-dwarf XUV stripping; the other major M-dwarf habitability
  hazard. Together with SolizWelsh, forms a two-pronged constraint on M-dwarf life.
- Ribas2015.txt — M-dwarf XUV evolution; provides the time-dependence of XUV hazard.
- GillisEtAl2026.txt — radius valley disappears around late M-dwarfs; suggests water-
  rich planets (not oxygen-accumulating), consistent with SolizWelsh's finding.
- Balbi2023.txt — the oxygen bottleneck threshold (18%); SolizWelsh explains why late
  M-dwarf HZ planets may never even approach this.

**Alternative photosynthesis:**
- Blankenship, R. E., et al. (2011) Science 332, 805 — "Comparing Photosynthetic and
  Photovoltaic Efficiencies and Recognizing the Potential for Improvement in Both."
  NIR-utilizing photosynthesis exists in some bacteria; these would not produce O₂.
- The existence of NIR photosynthesis does not resolve the PAR problem for oxygen
  accumulation — it would produce a biosphere without GOE.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### PAR gate for M-dwarf complex life
SolizWelsh2026 adds a photosynthetically active radiation gate to StarGen's
M-dwarf habitability model. It applies to the complex life and civilization
pipeline for M4+ dwarf host stars.

**PAR gate insertion in life pipeline (for M-dwarf planets):**
```csharp
// After Stage 4 (photosynthetic biosphere established):
// Check PAR sufficiency for oxygen accumulation
if (star.SpectralType == SpectralType.M && star.TemperatureK < 3600)
{
    double parGateProb = star.TemperatureK < 3200 ? 0.02 : 0.15;
    if (rng.NextDouble() > parGateProb)
    {
        // PAR insufficient for GOE on geological timescale
        planet.AtmosphereO2Fraction = 0.001;  // stays at trace levels
        planet.LifeStage = LifeStage.MicrobialOnly;
        return;  // civilization impossible via O₂ pathway
    }
}
```

**User lever:** `MDwarfPARGate` (enum: SolizWelsh2026 / Permissive / Disabled)

- Permissive: allow NIR photosynthesis pathway; reduce but don't eliminate probability
- Disabled: no PAR gate (for settings exploring alternative biology scenarios)
