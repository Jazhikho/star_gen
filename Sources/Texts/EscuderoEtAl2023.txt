## Abstract
Escudero and Amils review Earth's hard-rock deep subsurface biosphere and argue that
habitability assessments should include dark biospheres, not only surface liquid-water
worlds. The review emphasizes energy, liquid water, usable chemistry, and substrate
properties as requirements for deep subsurface life, with Mars exploration as a major
application.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Habitability is broader than surface HZ:** Surface liquid water is not the only
  meaningful habitat. Rocky planets and moons can support deep subsurface ecosystems.
- **Deep biosphere requirements:** The paper identifies energy, liquid water, suitable
  temperature, CHNOPS elements, and accessible rock/fluid interfaces as central.
- **Energy sources:** The hard-rock dark biosphere can rely on chemolithotrophic
  metabolisms, including hydrogen, sulfur, iron, methane, acetate, and radiolysis-linked
  pathways depending on geology.
- **Oxygen is not required:** Anaerobic metabolisms dominate many deep subsurface
  contexts, so oxygen-rich atmospheres should not be required for microbial habitability.
- **Mars relevance:** The paper treats hard-rock subsurface methods and biosignatures as
  important for Mars habitability assessment.
- **Limitation:** This is a review and method paper. It does not provide a single
  exoplanet probability model for subsurface life.

## StarGen applicability
Directly supports StarGen's distinction between surface habitability and subsurface or
dark-biosphere habitability. It is relevant to worlds with subsurface oceans, fractured
rock, geothermal or tidal energy, weak or absent atmospheres, and low surface irradiance.

Provides:
- A source anchor for `BiomeType.Subsurface`
- Support for life potential on airless or low-atmosphere bodies if water and energy exist
- A reason to keep microbial life separate from complex surface biosphere outcomes
- A framework for habitability readouts that do not over-focus on the classical HZ

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Subsurface habitability gate:**
```
subsurface_support =
    liquid_water_access
    * energy_flux
    * rock_fluid_interface
    * chemistry_access
```

**Generator rule implication:**
- `HasSubsurfaceOcean` should raise microbial-life support, but should not by itself
  imply photosynthetic, oxygenating, complex, or technosphere-supporting biospheres.
- Low atmosphere should not zero out simple-life support if subsurface water and energy
  are present.
- Tidal heating and geothermal proxies should be consumed by the biology evaluator.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Dark-biosphere branch
Current StarGen already has `CryosphereProps.HasSubsurfaceOcean`, `BiomeType.Subsurface`,
and a `BiologySupportEvaluator` branch that increases support for subsurface oceans and
tidal heating.

**Current fit:** moderate. The code recognizes subsurface oceans and non-surface life,
but the generator does not yet represent rock permeability, radiolytic hydrogen,
serpentinization, or geochemical energy availability as first-class fields.

**Recommended v1.0 disposition:** keep the current branch as a deterministic proxy, but
document it as StarGen tuning rather than a calibrated subsurface-biosphere model.
