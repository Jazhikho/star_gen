## Abstract
What have been the causes and consequences of technological evolution in world history?
In particular, what propels innovation and diffusion of military technologies, details of
which are comparatively well preserved and which are often seen as drivers of broad
socio-cultural processes? Here we analyze the evolution of key military technologies in
a sample of pre-industrial societies world-wide covering almost 10,000 years of history
using Seshat: Global History Databank. We empirically test previously speculative theories
that proposed world population size, connectivity between geographical areas of innovation
and adoption, and critical enabling technological advances (such as iron metallurgy and
horse riding) as central drivers of military technological evolution. We find that all of
these factors are strong predictors of change in military technology, whereas state-level
factors such as polity population, territorial size, or governance sophistication play no
major role. We discuss how our approach can be extended to explore technological change
more generally, and how our results carry important ramifications for understanding major
drivers of evolution of social complexity.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Global population size drives military tech:** Total world population at a given
  time is the strongest predictor of military technology index — more minds = more
  potential inventors and adopters. This is the "scale hypothesis" from Henrich (2004)
  and Kremer (1993).
- **Connectivity drives diffusion:** Geographic connectivity between regions (proximity,
  trade routes, common political sphere) accelerates both innovation AND adoption of
  military technologies. An isolated polity adopts military innovations slowly regardless
  of local population.
- **Critical enabling technologies ("gateway" techs):**
  - Iron metallurgy: massively expands weapon production scale and reduces cost
  - Horse riding: cavalry → rapid force projection, fundamentally changes warfare range
  - These are discontinuous enablers (phase transitions in military tech space), not
    gradual linear advances.
- **State-level factors are NOT major drivers:** Polity population, territorial size,
  and governance sophistication are POOR predictors of military technology innovation
  once global population and connectivity are controlled for. Big states adopt fast but
  don't innovate faster than small states.
- **Seshat: Global History Databank:** The empirical foundation is 10,000 years of
  cross-cultural data from ~400 societies. Military tech coded on a composite index
  including weapons, armor, projectiles, mounted warfare, naval tech.
- **Implication for "arms race" models:** Arms races between adjacent polities are
  secondary to the global technology pool. Polities adopt from the global pool, not
  primarily from bilateral competition.
- **Limitation:** Pre-industrial data only (Neolithic to Industrial Revolution).
  Industrial-era military tech (steam, gunpowder arms race, nuclear) may follow
  different dynamics.
- **Limitation:** Military technology is a proxy for general technological capability;
  the findings may not perfectly generalize to civilian tech.
- **Limitation:** Seshat coding involves expert judgment; some historical periods and
  regions are data-sparse.

## StarGen applicability
Provides the **technology diffusion model** for StarGen's civilization tech level
evolution. Key insights:
1. `TechLevel` increases with total sentient population in the accessible tech pool
   (not just local population) — isolation dramatically slows advancement
2. Gateway technologies (iron → steel → synthetic materials → FTL) create phase
   transitions in TL progression; these are threshold events, not linear
3. Connectivity (trade routes, jump lanes) is as important as population for
   tech advancement speed — reinforces StarGen's starport/trade importance

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Tech advancement model (global pool + connectivity):**
```csharp
// Tech advancement rate for a civilization depends on:
// 1. Size of the global accessible tech pool (total population in reachable network)
// 2. Connectivity to that pool (trade routes, starport class)
// 3. Gateway technology status (has the civ cleared critical enabling tech thresholds?)

double globalPoolSize = ComputeNetworkPopulation(civNetwork); // sum over reachable civs
double connectivity = ComputeConnectivity(starportClass, jumpLaneCount);

// Base innovation rate scales with log(global population) ~ Kremer/Turchin scaling
double innovationRate = Math.Log10(globalPoolSize / 1e6) * 0.02;  // per decade

// Connectivity multiplier: isolated civs get 0.1x; fully networked get 1.0x
double diffusionMultiplier = 0.1 + 0.9 * connectivity;

double techAdvanceRate = innovationRate * diffusionMultiplier;
```

**Gateway technology thresholds (phase transitions):**
Pre-industrial analogues → mapped to StarGen TechLevel equivalents:
```
Gateway Tech         | Pre-industrial | StarGen TL equivalent | Effect
--------------------|----------------|----------------------|------------------
Stone tools          | Paleolithic    | TL 0                 | Baseline
Metal smelting       | Bronze Age     | TL 2                 | 2x weapon capability
Iron metallurgy      | Iron Age       | TL 3                 | 3x production scale
Horse riding         | ~2000 BCE      | TL 2-3               | 5x force projection
Gunpowder            | Medieval       | TL 4-5               | Ranged dominance
Steam power          | Industrial     | TL 5                 | Economic multiplier
Nuclear              | Modern         | TL 8                 | Deterrence regime
Gravitic drive       | Spacefaring    | TL 9                 | Interplanetary scale
FTL                  | Interstellar   | TL 11+               | Network formation
```
Each gateway tech is a binary unlock, not a gradient. Modeled as a threshold effect.

**Polity size is NOT a primary driver (important constraint):**
```csharp
// DO NOT scale TechLevel directly with polityPopulation
// TechLevel should scale with networkPopulation (accessible global pool)
// This prevents large isolated worlds from being over-teched
// and small networked worlds from being under-teched
```

**Military tech → LawLevel connection:**
- Higher military tech enables more effective internal enforcement → can support
  higher LawLevel (more enforced rules)
- But: advanced military doesn't automatically mean high LawLevel — political will
  determines whether capability is used internally (AuerbachThachil2024 distinction)

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Military technology index → TechLevel component
TurchinEtAl2021 provides the empirical basis for how technological capability
accumulates across civilizations in StarGen — driven by network effects, not by
individual polity size.

**Network-scale tech advancement (core rule):**
```csharp
// TechLevel advancement is a function of the NETWORK, not the individual world
// Isolated world (no trade routes): slow advancement, cap at TL ~6-7
// Connected world (active jump lane): fast advancement, can reach TL 12+
// World with unique gateway tech: +1 to TL cap for the network as a whole

// Gateway tech unlock probability (per world, per generation):
// p_gateway = base_rate * (networkPopulation / 1e9) * connectivityIndex
double pGateway = 0.01 * Math.Log10(networkPop / 1e9 + 1) * connectivityIndex;
```

**TechLevel cap by connectivity:**
```
ConnectivityIndex | TL cap | Advancement rate
0.0 (isolated)    | 6      | Slow (centuries per TL step)
0.3 (one route)   | 9      | Moderate (decades per TL step)
0.7 (active hub)  | 12     | Fast (years per TL step)
1.0 (core world)  | 15+    | Very fast (continuous R&D)
```

**Gateway tech as phase transition:**
When a civilization acquires a gateway technology, TL increases by 1 immediately
and the TL cap for the entire connected network increases by 1.
This models the Turchin finding that critical enabling technologies cascade through
the entire connected network rapidly.

**User lever:** `TechDiffusionModel` (enum: NetworkScale / LocalPopulation / Random)
- NetworkScale: use Turchin network-population model (default; most scientifically grounded)
- LocalPopulation: use local polity population only (simpler, less realistic)
- Random: sample TL from distribution without mechanistic basis
