Source note for StarGen research review

Citation:
Pascucci, I., Testi, L., Herczeg, G. J., Long, F., Manara, C. F., Hendler, N., Mulders, G. D., Krijt, S., Ciesla, F., Henning, T., Mohanty, S., Drabek-Maunder, E., Apai, D., Szucs, L., Sacco, G., & Olofsson, J. (2016).
A steeper than linear disk mass-stellar mass scaling relation.
The Astrophysical Journal, 831(2), 125.
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03621
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/831/2/125

What was reviewed:
- Abstract and source metadata on 2026-04-22.

Key implementation notes used in StarGen:
- The paper presents an ALMA 887 micron survey of 93 disks in the approximately 2 Myr-old Chamaeleon I region, spanning host masses from about 2 to 0.03 solar masses.
- It finds that the dust-disk-mass to stellar-mass relation is steeper than linear, with power-law indices between about 1.3 and 1.9.
- The practical takeaway for StarGen is that solids budgets should scale with stellar mass more strongly than a simple linear or star-independent prior.

How StarGen should use this:
- As support for tying `SolidMassScalar` more directly to host-star mass or stellar scaffold class in `PlanetarySystemState`.
- As a calibration reminder that low-mass stars can still produce many small planets even while carrying smaller absolute solids budgets than solar-mass stars.
- As a source-backed reason to treat disk mass and planet count as related but not interchangeable parameters.
