Source note for StarGen research review

Citation:
Kopparapu, R. K., Ramirez, R. M., SchottelKotte, J., Kasting, J. F., Domagal-Goldman, S., & Eymet, V. (2014).
Habitable zones around main-sequence stars: Dependence on planetary mass.
The Astrophysical Journal Letters, 787(2), L29.
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5292
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/2041-8205/787/2/L29

What was reviewed:
- Abstract and source metadata on 2026-04-03.

Key implementation notes used in StarGen:
- The paper updates habitable-zone boundaries to account for planetary mass, showing that the same star does not imply one universal habitable band for every world.
- The habitable zone remains a useful high-level guide, but it should influence probability and constraints rather than act as a binary yes/no switch for biospheres.
- For StarGen, the practical takeaway is that stellar flux and habitable-zone alignment should matter to atmosphere, volatile retention, and biology-support scoring without becoming the only factor.

How StarGen uses this:
- As the basis for adding habitable-zone boundary fields and habitable-zone alignment into derived planetary and biology profiles.
- As support for treating biosphere support as a weighted function of flux, orbit, atmosphere, and moon heating rather than a pure human-habitability shortcut.
