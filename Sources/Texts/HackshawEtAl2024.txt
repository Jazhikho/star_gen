## Abstract
This paper presents a detailed chemical cartography of the Galactic disk using giant
stars from APOGEE DR17, focusing on mapping azimuthal variations in multiple elemental
abundances ([X/Fe] for X = Fe, Mg, O, and others) on top of the well-known radial
metallicity gradient. The study confirms a radial metallicity gradient of
Δ[Fe/H]/ΔR = −0.066 ± 0.0004 dex/kpc and a vertical gradient of
Δ[Fe/H]/ΔZ = −0.164 ± 0.001 dex/kpc. In addition, the authors find azimuthal
variations at the ±0.1 dex level in [Fe/H] and ±0.05 dex in alpha-element ratios
([Mg/Fe], [O/Fe]). These azimuthal variations are age-dependent: older stellar
populations show larger deviations from the mean radial gradient. The results
demonstrate that the Milky Way disk is not axisymmetric in its chemical abundance
distribution, with spiral arm and bar-driven mixing as likely causes.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- Radial [Fe/H] gradient: −0.066 ± 0.0004 dex/kpc (most precise measurement to date
  from APOGEE; confirmed over 4–14 kpc range)
- Vertical [Fe/H] gradient: −0.164 ± 0.001 dex/kpc (strong; disk chemistry stratified
  with height above plane)
- Azimuthal [Fe/H] scatter: ±0.1 dex around the mean radial gradient at any given radius
  (comparable to scatter in the thin disk's metallicity distribution function)
- Alpha-element azimuthal scatter: ±0.05 dex ([Mg/Fe], [O/Fe]) — smaller but significant
- Age dependence: older stellar populations (>8 Gyr) show larger azimuthal deviations;
  young populations track the spiral arm structure more tightly
- Non-axisymmetry likely driven by: spiral arms, bar resonances, open clusters,
  recent star formation episodes (not just measurement noise)
- Limitation: APOGEE samples giant stars; may not represent the full thin-disk age/
  metallicity structure perfectly (evolved off the main sequence, sampling biases)
- Limitation: only traces stars currently in the disk; radial migration means some
  stars are not near their birth location
- Limitation: azimuthal variations are at the ±0.1 dex level — small but real; may
  not be important for many StarGen use cases

## StarGen applicability
Primary empirical reference for the Milky Way's radial metallicity gradient. The value
−0.066 dex/kpc is the most precise recent determination and should be used as the
default `metallicity_gradient_dex_per_kpc` in StarGen's galaxy generator. The azimuthal
scatter provides a justification for adding a stochastic metallicity term at any given
location. Cited in GalaxyScienceReferenceCatalog.cs for `metallicity_gradient_dex_per_kpc`.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Default metallicity gradient for StarGen galaxy generator:**
- d[Fe/H]/dR = −0.066 dex/kpc (radial; use as the slope of the linear metallicity
  model in the disk)
- Reference point: [Fe/H] = 0.00 at R = 8.0 kpc (solar neighborhood calibration)
- This gives [Fe/H](R) = −0.066 × (R_kpc − 8.0) for a first-order model

**[Fe/H] at key radii (from gradient):**
- R = 4 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ +0.26 dex
- R = 6 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ +0.13 dex
- R = 8 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ 0.00 dex (solar)
- R = 10 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ −0.13 dex
- R = 12 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ −0.26 dex
- R = 14 kpc: [Fe/H] ≈ −0.40 dex

**Vertical gradient:**
- d[Fe/H]/dZ = −0.164 dex/kpc (perpendicular to disk plane)
- At Z = 0: use radial gradient value; at Z = 1 kpc above plane: subtract 0.164 dex
- Relevant for generating stars in thick disk or halo context

**Azimuthal scatter (stochastic term):**
- At any (R, Z), add a Gaussian scatter with σ_az ≈ 0.05–0.10 dex to [Fe/H]
  to represent azimuthal non-uniformity
- Optionally correlate with stellar age: σ_az ∝ age (larger for older stars)
- Default: apply σ_az = 0.08 dex if `AzimuthalChemicalVariation` is enabled

**Implications for planet habitability:**
- The ±0.1 dex azimuthal scatter means that at a given R, some regions are ~26% more
  metal-rich (0.1 dex) and others are ~26% more metal-poor than the mean
- This scatter can shift individual stellar metallicities across the GHZ metallicity
  threshold ([Fe/H] = −0.5 dex) for stars near the outer disk boundary

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**Companion and cross-check papers:**
- TrentinEtAl2024.txt (C-MetaLL VI): independent Cepheid-based measurement of the
  radial [Fe/H] gradient gives −0.071 dex/kpc — consistent with Hackshaw et al.'s
  −0.066 dex/kpc within ~1σ; together these two anchor the default StarGen value.
- SpitoniEtAl2017.txt: chemical evolution model predicts a similar gradient from
  theory; this paper provides the observational calibration.

**Earlier APOGEE metallicity gradient papers (context for evolution of the measurement):**
- Hayden, M. R., et al. (2014) — APOGEE DR10 gradient: −0.056 dex/kpc. arXiv:1408.6855.
- Donor, J., et al. (2020) — open cluster metallicity gradient with APOGEE: −0.068 dex/kpc.
  arXiv:1903.01653.
- These confirm the Hackshaw et al. (2024) value is consistent with the literature
  trend; the precision is improved by DR17's larger sample.

**Non-axisymmetry / azimuthal variation literature:**
- Anders, F., et al. (2017) — early evidence for azimuthal metallicity variations from
  APOGEE: arXiv:1604.07537.
- Bovy, J., et al. (2019) — APOGEE age-metallicity-spatial structure analysis.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Radial metallicity gradient — default calibration
The −0.066 dex/kpc gradient from Hackshaw et al. (2024) is the recommended default
for StarGen's `metallicity_gradient_dex_per_kpc` parameter (in GalaxyScienceReferenceCatalog.cs).

**Implementation:**
```csharp
// Linear disk metallicity model
double diskFeH(double r_kpc, double centerFeH = 0.264) =>
    centerFeH + MetallicityGradientDexPerKpc * (r_kpc - 8.0);
// MetallicityGradientDexPerKpc default: -0.066
// (yields [Fe/H] = 0.00 at r = 8.0 kpc by construction)
```

**Azimuthal scatter (optional, controlled by user lever):**
```csharp
// Add azimuthal stochastic component if enabled
if (EnableAzimuthalChemicalVariation)
{
    double sigma = 0.08; // dex; from Hackshaw et al. ±0.1 dex range
    double deltaFeH = rng.NextGaussian(0.0, sigma);
    feH += deltaFeH;
}
```

**Vertical gradient hook (for thick disk / halo context):**
```csharp
double verticalCorrection = VerticalMetallicityGradientDexPerKpc * Math.Abs(z_kpc);
// VerticalMetallicityGradientDexPerKpc default: -0.164
feH += verticalCorrection;
```

**User levers:** `MetallicityGradientDexPerKpc` (default −0.066), `CentralDiskFeH`
(default +0.264), `EnableAzimuthalChemicalVariation` (bool, default false for
deterministic modes), `VerticalMetallicityGradientDexPerKpc` (default −0.164).

**Cross-references:** TrentinEtAl2024.txt (cross-check), SpitoniEtAl2017.txt (theory
backbone), ForgnEtAl2017.txt (GHZ uses this gradient as input),
BlandHawthornGerhard2016.txt (MW disk scale height/length context).
