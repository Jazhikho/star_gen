Source note for StarGen research review

Citation:
Petigura, E. A., Howard, A. W., & Marcy, G. W. (2013).
Prevalence of Earth-size planets orbiting Sun-like stars.
Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 110(48), 19273-19278.
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6806
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319909110

What was reviewed:
- Abstract and source metadata on 2026-04-22.

Key implementation notes used in StarGen:
- The paper reports that 11 +/- 4% of Sun-like stars harbor an Earth-size planet receiving between one and four times Earth's incident flux.
- It also reports that Earth-size-planet occurrence is approximately constant with increasing orbital period in equal intervals of logP out to about 200 days.
- The practical takeaway for StarGen is that Earth-size rocky planets in temperate irradiance regimes should not be treated as rare edge cases around Sun-like stars.

How StarGen should use this:
- As a demographic anchor for `SystemPlanetGenerator` and the aggregate planetary priors when weighting terrestrial outcomes in the inner and temperate system.
- As support for keeping terrestrial-planet occurrence broadly distributed across log-period space instead of over-concentrating rocky worlds into one narrow orbital band.
- As a calibration target for future occurrence tests that compare temperate rocky-world frequency against Sun-like host expectations.
