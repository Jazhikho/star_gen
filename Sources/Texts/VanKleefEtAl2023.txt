## Abstract
Van Kleef et al. examine how norm violations affect dominance, prestige, and voluntary
influence granting. Across five studies, they find that norm violations are associated
with dominance, while norm abidance is associated with prestige. When local group norms
and wider community norms conflict, actors who violate global norms while abiding by
local group norms can gain prestige and leadership endorsement.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Dominance route:** Norm violation can signal dominance because violators appear
  willing or able to risk sanctions.
- **Prestige route:** Norm abidance often supports prestige because it signals value to
  the group and conformity to locally valued expectations.
- **Nested norms matter:** Local group norms and global community norms can conflict.
- **Rebels with a cause:** Violating global norms while abiding by local norms can
  increase leadership endorsement when the conflict makes the act seem locally valuable.
- **Influence granting:** Prestige generally supports voluntary influence more than raw
  dominance, but context changes the effect.
- **Limitation:** This is contemporary human social psychology. It is not a direct
  government, law, or alien-culture generator.
