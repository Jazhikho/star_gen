## Abstract
We present thermal infrared observations of 164 cometary bodies observed during the
WISE and NEOWISE surveys. Using the Near-Earth Asteroid Thermal Model (NEATM), we
derive nucleus radii, geometric albedos, and in many cases activity levels (as
characterized by the active fraction — the fraction of the surface area that must be
sublimating to produce the observed flux). The sample includes Jupiter-family comets
(JFCs), nearly isotropic comets (NICs, including long-period comets and Halley-type
comets), and Damocloids. Key results: (1) JFC nuclei have a median radius of ~0.6 km
with a steep size distribution; (2) cometary nuclei have characteristically low albedos
(median p_v ~ 0.04 for JFCs); (3) activity levels vary widely but the active fraction
is typically 1–10% of the nuclear surface; (4) nucleus size and albedo distributions
differ between JFCs and NICs. This survey represents the most comprehensive thermal
IR characterization of the cometary nucleus population to date.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Median JFC nucleus radius:** ~0.6 km (range 0.1–5 km; a few outliers to ~10 km)
- **Albedo:** median p_v ~ 0.04 for JFCs; median p_v ~ 0.04–0.06 for NICs.
  Cometary nuclei are among the darkest objects in the solar system.
- **Size distribution:** JFC nuclei follow a differential power-law size distribution
  with slope q ~ -2.7 to -3.0 (cumulative slope ~ -1.7 to -2.0) for radii > 0.3 km.
  This is significantly shallower than the small-end asteroid distribution.
- **Active fraction:** Median ~3% of nuclear surface area is actively sublimating.
  Wide range: some comets have <0.1% active area, others >50%.
- **JFC vs NIC:** JFCs (short-period, dynamically evolved) have a distinct size/albedo
  distribution compared to NICs (dynamically fresh, often larger nuclei). NICs tend to
  have slightly larger radii and comparable albedos.
- **Damocloids (inactive HTC-like objects):** Albedos similar to comets, consistent
  with dormant or extinct cometary nuclei.
- **Methodology (NEATM):** Near-Earth Asteroid Thermal Model applied to WISE W3
  (12 μm) and W4 (22 μm) photometry. The beam correction factor η accounts for
  non-isotropic thermal emission; median η ~ 1.03 for comets.
- **Limitation:** Many comets observed at low activity; nucleus size is best constrained
  for objects where the coma is faint relative to the nucleus. High-activity objects
  require coma subtraction with associated systematic uncertainties.
- **Limitation:** The NEATM assumes a spherical, non-rotating nucleus. Shape effects and
  rotation can introduce systematic biases at the ~10–20% level.
- **Limitation:** Active fraction estimate depends on assumed sublimation model (pure water
  ice with standard latent heat). For CO₂- or CO-driven activity, the model is modified.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**JFC nucleus radius distribution:**
```
// Log-normal fit to WISE JFC sample (approximate):
// Median r = 0.6 km; σ_log = 0.45 dex
double r_nucleus_km = Math.Pow(10, rng.NextGaussian(Math.Log10(0.6), 0.45));
r_nucleus_km = Math.Clamp(r_nucleus_km, 0.1, 15.0);
```

**NIC (long-period comet) nucleus radius:**
```
// Slightly larger median; fewer well-measured objects
// Median r ~ 1.0 km; σ_log ~ 0.5 dex (more uncertain)
double r_nic_km = Math.Pow(10, rng.NextGaussian(Math.Log10(1.0), 0.5));
```

**Albedo:**
```
// JFC: p_v ~ 0.04 (very low; essentially uniform at this precision)
// Use Gaussian: mean=0.04, sigma=0.015, clamp [0.02, 0.12]
double albedo = rng.NextGaussian(0.04, 0.015);
albedo = Math.Clamp(albedo, 0.02, 0.12);
```

**Active fraction (if coma modeled):**
```
// Log-normal centered at 0.03 (3%)
// active_frac in [0.001, 0.8] (some comets nearly fully active)
double log_af = rng.NextGaussian(Math.Log10(0.03), 0.7);
double active_frac = Math.Clamp(Math.Pow(10, log_af), 0.001, 0.8);
```

**Cometary subtype classification:**
- JFC (P < 20 yr, TJ > 2): use JFC distributions above
- HTC / Halley-type (20 < P < 200 yr): use NIC distributions
- LPC (P > 200 yr): use NIC distributions; nucleus may be larger (dynamically fresh)
- Damocloid (TJ < 2, no observed coma): treat as dormant JFC/HTC; same albedo prior

**Size distribution power-law slope:**
For generating a population of N comets in a simulated system:
- Differential slope: dN/dr ∝ r^(-3.0) for r > 0.3 km (JFCs)
- Normalization: based on observed JFC population (~400 known JFCs with r > 1 km
  in the inner solar system as of 2017 WISE census)

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**This paper supersedes:**
- LamyEtAl2004 (Comets II book chapter) — now deprecated in this corpus.
  Bauer et al. (2017) covers a much larger sample with uniform thermal IR methodology.

**Complementary nucleus work:**
- Fernandez, Y. R., et al. (2013) Icarus 226, 1138 — Spitzer thermal properties and
  nucleus lifetimes for JFC family. arXiv:1303.4098. Precedes this paper; still useful
  for thermal inertia and physical aging constraints.
- Kokotanekova, R., et al. (2017) A&A 603, A92 — TRAPPIST survey of JFC nucleus
  rotation and sizes; complements WISE with visible-wavelength data. arXiv:1703.07650.
- Snodgrass, C., et al. (2011) A&A 557, A33 — size distribution of JFC nuclei from VLT
  survey; good cross-check on size distribution slope. arXiv:1110.1701.

**Post-2017 updates:**
- Bauer et al. (2021) and subsequent NEOWISE reactivation papers — extended sample
  through mission continuation. Check ADS for updates to this specific paper's sample.
- Lauer et al. (2022) — Pluto flyby era updates on KBO surface albedo and nucleus
  properties (relevant for transition between KBOs and JFC source population).
- Marsset et al. (2022) — nucleus properties from ground-based survey.

```csharp
// 1. Determine dynamical type from orbital elements
CometType cometType = ClassifyCometType(period, tisserandParameter);

// 2. Sample nucleus radius from type-appropriate distribution

    // JFC:  LogNormal(μ=log10(0.6), σ=0.45), clamp [0.1, 15]
    // NIC:  LogNormal(μ=log10(1.0), σ=0.50), clamp [0.1, 30]

    // Gaussian(μ=0.04, σ=0.015), clamp [0.02, 0.12]

// r = sqrt(1329 / sqrt(p_v) / 10^(H/5)) km  [standard H-mag relation]

    // LogNormal(μ=log10(0.03), σ=0.7), clamp [0.001, 0.8]
```
