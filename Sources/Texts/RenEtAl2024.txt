## Abstract
Selecting suitable trading partners in the globalized trade landscape remains critical.
Traditional selection processes driven by factors like comparative advantage and trade
costs are cumbersome and incomplete. Economic complexity offers a more precise measure
of a country's economic development and product capacity, facilitating future-oriented
choices. Leveraging international trade data from 2001 to 2015, this paper employs
economic complexity theories to analyze Chinese and US trade networks in the global
market. It explores multi-level considerations for exporting countries' partner selection,
revealing varying product diversification challenges and market clustering tendencies.
China shows increasing overall product proximity relationships, while the US exhibits
decreasing product proximities. Countries like Japan and Germany maintain stable trade
positions, while others fluctuate. Countries like Nigeria, Bangladesh, and Cambodia have
higher positions in the Chinese market, suggesting promising long-term developmental
relationships.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Economic complexity as a trade partner selection criterion:** ECI (Hidalgo &
  Hausmann 2009) predicts which trade partnerships are durable and mutually beneficial
  better than raw comparative advantage measures.
- **Product space proximity as a clustering tool:** Products cluster into network
  communities (textile cluster, machinery cluster, chemicals cluster). Stable long-term
  trade partners share product-space proximity — they export complementary product
  categories.
- **China vs. US trade networks:** China's network shows increasing product proximity
  and clustering (growing economic complexity); US shows decreasing proximity (service
  economy shift, less manufacturing export diversity).
- **Asymmetric partnership stability:** Low-complexity countries (Nigeria, Bangladesh,
  Cambodia in the paper) benefit from ties to high-complexity partners because they
  can move into adjacent product spaces. The high-complexity partner benefits from
  access to labor-intensive production.
- **Implication for StarGen trade:** A world's trade partners should be selected based
  on ECI complementarity — high-complexity worlds trade technology for raw materials
  from lower-complexity worlds. This generates natural trade flow patterns.
- **Limitation:** The study covers 2001-2015 Earth data only. The product space
  concepts are general but the specific ECI values and clusters are Earth-specific.
- **Limitation:** Physical trade in a space-faring civilization has different cost
  structures (distance matters less for high-value goods; mass matters more). The
  product-space model may need adaptation.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Trade partner selection from ECI:**
```csharp
// For each potential trade pair (world A, world B):
// Trade probability is highest when:
// 1. ECI values are different (complementarity)
// 2. Product space proximity is high (compatible product categories)
// 3. Geographic distance is within jump range (connectivity)
double tradeScore = ECI_Complementarity(worldA.ECI, worldB.ECI)
                  * ProductProximity(worldA.ExportBasket, worldB.ExportBasket)
                  * JumpRangeWeight(worldA.Location, worldB.Location);
// High ECI + Low ECI pair → highest trade score (classic tech-for-resources)
// Two high ECI worlds → moderate trade score (differentiated manufactured goods)
// Two low ECI worlds → low trade score (limited capacity to trade)
```

**Trade flow direction:**
```
High ECI (TL 9+) → Low ECI (TL 0-5): exports manufactured goods, technology, services
Low ECI (TL 0-5) → High ECI (TL 9+): exports raw materials, agricultural products, labor
Moderate ECI similar → both ways: differentiated manufactured goods, specialization
```

**Trade code assignment based on ECI + resources:**
```
ECI range  | Primary exports (Trade codes)
0.0-0.3    | Raw materials: Ag, Mi, Na, Ni
0.3-0.5    | Manufactured goods: In, Re
0.5-0.7    | Advanced manufacturing: In, Ht (emerging)
0.7-0.9    | High tech: Ht, Ri, Ph
0.9-1.0    | Services + high tech: Ri, Ht, Di (diverse)
```
