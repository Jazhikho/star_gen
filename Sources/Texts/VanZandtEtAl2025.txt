## Abstract
We present the results of the Distant Giants Survey, a three-year radial velocity (RV)
campaign to search for wide-separation giant planets orbiting Sun-like stars known to host
an inner transiting planet. We defined a distant giant to have a = 1–10 AU and Mp sin i =
70–4000 M⊕ (0.2–12.5 MJ), and required transiting planets to have a < 1 AU and Rp =
1–4 R⊕. We assembled our sample of 47 stars using a single selection function, and observed
each star at monthly intervals to obtain ~30 RV observations per target. The final catalog
includes a total of twelve distant companions: four giant planets detected during our survey,
two previously known giant planets, and six objects of uncertain disposition identified
through RV/astrometric accelerations. Statistically, half of the uncertain objects are
planets and the remainder are stars/brown dwarfs. We calculated target-by-target
completeness maps to account for missed planets. We found evidence for a moderate
enhancement of distant giants (DG) in the presence of close-in small planets (CS):
P(DG|CS) = 30 +14/−12%, over the field rate of P(DG) = 16 +2/−2%. No enhancement is
disfavored at p ~ 8%. We found no evidence that stellar metallicity enhances P(DG|CS).
We found evidence that distant giant companions are preferentially found in systems with
multiple transiting planets and have lower eccentricities than randomly selected giant
planets. This points toward dynamically cool formation pathways for the giants that do not
disturb the inner systems.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **P(distant giant | inner small planets) = 30%:** Stars with inner close-in rocky/sub-
  Neptune planets have roughly double the chance of also hosting an outer giant (1–10 AU)
  compared to the field rate (~16%). This moderate conditional enhancement (~1.9×)
  suggests a genuine formation-level correlation.
- **Field rate P(distant giant) = 16%:** Without conditioning on inner planets, ~16% of
  Sun-like stars host at least one giant planet at 1–10 AU. This is the base rate for
  a "Jupiter analog" in the system.
- **"Dynamically cool" formation pathway:** Distant giants in systems with inner small
  planets have lower eccentricities than field giant planets. This is inconsistent with
  dynamical sculpting (which would disrupt inner planets) and consistent with: (a) smooth
  disk migration without scattering, and/or (b) both giant and small planets forming in
  the same dynamically calm protoplanetary disk.
- **Multi-planet systems preferred:** Systems with multiple transiting inner planets are
  more likely to have a distant giant companion. Single-planet inner systems are less
  enhanced.
- **No metallicity correlation:** Unlike hot Jupiters (which strongly prefer metal-rich
  stars), the distant giant enhancement does not correlate with stellar metallicity.
  This implies a different formation pathway from hot Jupiter progenitors.
- **Good Jupiter implication:** The moderate enhancement supports the "Good Jupiter"
  concept (Wetherill 1994; ForganRice2010 criterion 3): outer giants at moderate
  distances may be beneficial (or at least not destructive) for inner system stability.
  Low-eccentricity distant giants do not disrupt inner systems.
- **Limitation:** Small sample (47 stars, 12 distant companions). Uncertainties are large;
  P(DG|CS) posterior has +14/-12% uncertainty.
- **Limitation:** Selection on known inner transiting planets introduces a detection bias.
  Systems with distant giants but no inner transiting planets are not included.
- **Limitation:** 3-year RV baseline; giants at 5–10 AU may be underrepresented (orbital
  period up to ~30 years — only partial RV coverage).

  "stopped in time" outcome.

**Outstanding question:**
VanZandt finds that distant giants in inner-planet systems have low eccentricities.
This raises the question: which came first, the giant or the small planets? The
formation sequence matters for StarGen's generative ordering. The most likely
interpretation (dynamically cool disk) supports generating the giant at the same time
as the inner planet disk, not sequentially.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Outer giant as a system architecture parameter
VanZandtEtAl2025 elevates the outer giant (Jupiter analog) from an incidental element
to a system-level architectural parameter that correlates with the inner planet population.
Rather than placing an outer giant independently, StarGen should condition its probability
on the inner planet count and set its eccentricity to match the dynamically cool profile.

**Primary updates to StarGen system generator:**
1. Move outer giant probability from a flat 16% to a conditional rate based on inner
   planet count: 30% for 1+ inner planets, boosted for 2+ inner planets
2. When outer giant co-exists with inner small planets: sample eccentricity from
   low-eccentricity distribution (e < 0.20)
3. Update ForganRice criterion 3: "Good Jupiter" (outer, low-e) is neutral-to-beneficial;
   only hot Jupiter (inward migrated) is the failing case
