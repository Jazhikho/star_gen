## Abstract
This article addresses the interstate differences in outcomes from the COVID-19 pandemic
by focusing on state capacity. State capacity refers to states' ability to create and implement
policy. We posit that states want to limit death and destruction within their borders. COVID-19
created an instance in which states had a shared, preferred outcome but had very different
levels of success. Using a novel measure of state capacity that allows for subnational
comparisons — and is independent of ideological political will — we show that states with
greater capacity experienced fewer excess deaths during 2020 and more successfully
distributed vaccines in early 2021. The findings are robust to various measures of
partisanship, social capital, geography, and demographics.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **State capacity predicts policy outcomes independently of ideology:** States with
  higher administrative/bureaucratic capacity achieved better COVID-19 outcomes
  regardless of political partisanship. Capacity and political will are separable dimensions.
- **Novel state capacity measure:** The paper constructs a US state-level capacity index
  combining economic freedom, democratic accountability, and bureaucratic effectiveness.
  This three-component measure is validated against pandemic outcomes.
- **Dimensions of state capacity (from the paper's index framework):**
  1. Bureaucratic/administrative capacity — ability to implement decisions
  2. Fiscal capacity — ability to raise and deploy resources
  3. Legal-coercive capacity — ability to enforce compliance
- **Key result:** The capacity index has r ~ 0.5–0.6 with excess-death outcomes;
  this is comparable to or exceeds the predictive power of partisanship.
- **Limitation:** The study is confined to US states (subnational variation within a
  single federal system). Generalizability to cross-national variation is uncertain.
- **Limitation:** COVID-19 is an unusual crisis. State capacity in normal-governance
  conditions may be structured differently than in emergency response.
- **Limitation:** The capacity index is contemporary and backward-looking (based on
  institutional track record). New states, post-revolution states, or externally-formed
  states may have different trajectories.

## StarGen applicability
**Assessment: partially on-topic.** The paper's direct subject (COVID response) is
off-topic for StarGen. However, the three-component state capacity framework and the
separation of "capacity" from "political will" are directly applicable to StarGen's
government modeling:
- `LawLevel` in Traveller/SWN maps to legal-coercive capacity
- Government type maps to political will / ideology
- `TechLevel` and economic development map to fiscal capacity
- The paper provides validation that these three dimensions are empirically separable,
  supporting StarGen's multi-axis civilization model.

**Recommendation:** Use for the conceptual three-axis government model only.
Do not cite COVID outcomes in StarGen documentation. Cross-reference with
MurphyEtAl2023 (open access orders index) and HarfstEtAl2024 (political legitimacy).

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Three-axis government model (from state capacity dimensions):**
```
Axis 1: Administrative capacity   → maps to LawLevel (ability to enforce rules)
Axis 2: Fiscal capacity           → maps to TechLevel / economic complexity
Axis 3: Democratic legitimacy     → maps to GovernmentType (authority basis)
```

**Key insight for StarGen — capacity ≠ political form:**
- A military dictatorship can have high administrative capacity (enforces rules well)
  and low democratic legitimacy → high LawLevel, authoritarian GovernmentType
- A democracy can have low administrative capacity → low effective LawLevel despite
  formal rule-of-law
- This justifies independent sampling of GovernmentType and LawLevel in StarGen

**State capacity vs. political will — independence:**

## StarGen mechanics translation

### State capacity as a civilization generation parameter
AuerbachThachil2024 supports treating administrative capacity, fiscal capacity, and
political legitimacy as three orthogonal axes in StarGen's government generation.

**Government profile sampling (three-axis model):**
```csharp
public struct GovernmentProfile
{
    public double AdminCapacity;    // 0-1: ability to implement policy
    public double FiscalCapacity;   // 0-1: ability to raise/deploy resources
    public double Legitimacy;       // 0-1: degree of consent-based authority
}

// Sample each with mild correlations between them
GovernmentProfile SampleGovernment(GalaxyRng rng, CivilizationContext ctx)
{
    double adminBase = rng.NextGaussian(0.5, 0.2);
    double fiscalBase = adminBase * 0.5 + rng.NextGaussian(0.5, 0.2) * 0.5;
    double legBase = rng.NextGaussian(0.5, 0.25);  // nearly independent
    return new GovernmentProfile
    {
        AdminCapacity = Math.Clamp(adminBase, 0.0, 1.0),
        FiscalCapacity = Math.Clamp(fiscalBase, 0.0, 1.0),
        Legitimacy = Math.Clamp(legBase, 0.0, 1.0)
    };
}

// Map to RPG outputs:
// LawLevel: primarily AdminCapacity (0→LL0; 1→LL9)
// GovernmentType: primarily Legitimacy dimension
// TechLevel: partially FiscalCapacity
```
