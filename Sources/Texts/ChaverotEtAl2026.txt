## Abstract
This chapter provides a non-exhaustive overview of climate simulations, habitability,
and biosignature detection for terrestrial exoplanets, with a focus on science produced
within the NCCR PlanetS program (2014-2025). The review covers: (1) the concept of
habitability for Solar System objects and exoplanets and how it is assessed; (2) the concept
of biosignatures and how they may be detected by current and future instruments. Habitability
is reviewed in terms of five key factors: presence of liquid water, energy source, essential
CHNOPS nutrients, protection from harmful conditions, and long-term stability. The review
covers M-dwarf exoplanets (TRAPPIST-1 system, Proxima Centauri b) currently accessible
to JWST, as well as Solar System targets (Mars, Europa, Enceladus, Titan). Upcoming
instruments discussed include RISTRETTO, ANDES/PCS at the ELT, the LIFE large
interferometer, and the Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO).

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Habitability five-factor framework:** Liquid water (limiting factor), energy source,
  CHNOPS nutrients, protection from harmful radiation, and long-term stability.
  Surface requirement is also noted: interfaces between phases (surface/ocean) allow
  chemical complexity to build up.
- **M-dwarf planet observational focus:** TRAPPIST-1 planets and Proxima-b are the
  near-term test cases for atmospheric characterization. Tidal locking (permanent day/
  night sides) fundamentally changes climate compared to Earth-like rotation.
- **Biosignature detection pipeline:**
  JWST (transmission/emission spectroscopy now) →
  RISTRETTO/ANDES at ELT (direct imaging, reflected light) →
  LIFE interferometer (mid-IR thermal emission, ~2030s) →
  HWO (UV-optical-NIR direct imaging, ~2040s)
- **CHNOPS nutrients:** Carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and sulfur are
  the essential building blocks. Phosphorus is particularly constrained — derived
  primarily from continental weathering (consistent with SpohnEtAl2026).
- **Only one data point (Earth):** The review notes that all habitability definitions
  are necessarily Earth-analogous. Life-as-we-know-it is the only operationalizable
  framework; truly alien life may be undetectable with current approaches.
- **Solar System proxies:** Mars (geological habitability record), Europa (liquid water
  under ice), Enceladus (active vent chemistry), Titan (complex organics) provide
  diversity of habitability types beyond classical HZ.
- **Limitation:** This is a review chapter, not a primary research paper. It synthesizes
  the NCCR PlanetS research portfolio and does not introduce new original findings.
  Use for context and instrument timelines, not for novel quantitative constraints.
- **Limitation:** Author list is very large (21 authors); the "et al." in the file name
  does not correctly reflect that Bhatnagar is first author, not Chaverot.

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Habitability five-factor checklist (Chaverot et al. 2026, citing Meadows & Barnes 2018):**
```csharp
// Five-factor habitability evaluation
bool habCheck = planet.HasLiquidWater           // factor 1
    && planet.HasEnergySources                   // factor 2 (stellar, geothermal, chemical)
    && planet.HasCHNOPSNutrients                 // factor 3 (continental weathering key for P)
    && planet.IsProtectedFromRadiation           // factor 4 (magnetic field, ozone)
    && planet.HasLongTermClimateStability;       // factor 5 (CO2 weathering feedback)
planet.IsHabitable = habCheck;
```

**Tidal locking modifier for M-dwarf habitable zone planets:**
```csharp
// Tidal locking is common for close-in HZ M-dwarf planets
// Affects habitability through day/night temperature asymmetry
bool tidallyLocked = star.SpectralType == SpectralType.M
    && planet.OrbitalPeriodDays < 50.0;  // rough threshold for strong tidal locking

if (tidallyLocked)
{
    planet.ClimateModifier = ClimateModifier.TidallyLocked;
    // Reduces effective habitability by ~30% due to atmospheric collapse risk
    planet.HabitabilityScore *= 0.70;
}
```

**Biosignature detectability window (instrument timeline):**
```
Era      | Instrument      | Detection capability
---------|-----------------|----------------------------------
Current  | JWST            | Transmission spectroscopy (thick atmospheres only)
~2030s   | ELT + RISTRETTO | Reflected light spectra (bright nearby targets)
~2030s   | LIFE            | Mid-IR thermal emission (multiple targets)
~2040s   | HWO             | UV-optical-NIR direct imaging (Earth twins)
```

This timeline informs when, in-universe, a civilization might detect biosignatures
from another world — feeds into the "detectable by peer civilization" flag.

new mechanical contribution is the explicit tidal locking habitability modifier for
close-in M-dwarf HZ planets, which reduces effective habitability score by ~30% due to
