## Abstract
According to the "hard-steps" model, the origin of humanity required "successful passage
through a number of intermediate steps" (so-called "hard steps") that were intrinsically
improbable in the time available for biological evolution on Earth. This model similarly
predicts an extremely low probability for the emergence of technological intelligence on
other planets. In this paper we reassess the hard-steps model in light of modern
astrobiology. We argue that humans — and analogous life beyond Earth — may represent
the probable outcome of biological and planetary co-evolution, rather than an extremely
improbable sequence of accidents. We present evidence from Earth's fossil and genomic
records, comparative biology, and co-evolutionary theory suggesting that many so-called
"hard steps" are better understood as the inevitable outcomes of positive feedbacks
between life and environment — including, critically, the Great Oxidation Event and the
subsequent accumulation of atmospheric oxygen. We propose that the hard-steps model
significantly underestimates the probability of technological intelligence when planetary
co-evolution is properly accounted for. Humans may represent a probable — not improbable —
outcome, given the right planetary conditions.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Challenge to the hard-steps model:** Carter (1983) and Hanson (1998) argued that
  each major evolutionary "step" toward human-like intelligence was an independently
  improbable accident. The product of many small probabilities → extraordinarily rare
  intelligence. Mills et al. argue this underestimates evolutionary constraints and
  co-evolutionary dynamics.
- **Key reinterpretation — co-evolution not contingency:**
  - The Great Oxidation Event (GOE) was not a lucky accident; it was the mechanistically
    inevitable result of oxygenic photosynthesis + sufficient geological time for oxygen
    sinks to be filled. This removes the GOE as a "hard step."
  - Eukaryotic cell origin (endosymbiosis) may be less improbable than thought; similar
    organelle-acquisition events may have occurred multiple times on Earth.
  - Multicellularity evolved independently 25+ times — not a single improbable event.
  - Nervous systems evolved independently multiple times (bilateral, cnidarian, cephalopod).
- **Planetary co-evolution is the key:** Life does not just adapt to environments;
  it transforms them in ways that enable further evolutionary transitions. The biosphere
  co-evolves with the planet. Once life-environment feedbacks are engaged, subsequent
  steps may be more probable than in a passive planetary environment.
- **Residual "true hard steps":** The paper does not eliminate all hard steps. The
  authors acknowledge that the origin of life itself (abiogenesis) and perhaps the
  eukaryote-to-multicellular transition may remain genuinely improbable steps. But the
  number of hard steps is reduced compared to the Carter/Hanson model.
- **Revised probability estimate:** The paper does not give a specific numerical estimate
  for p(intelligence). Instead it argues the probability is substantially higher than
  the hard-steps model predicts, and that planets which develop oxygenic photosynthesis
  and accumulate oxygen to the 18%+ threshold (Balbi2023.txt level) may have a
  substantially elevated probability of developing complex cognition.
- **Connection to Balbi2023:** The oxygen accumulation pathway (GOE → complex life →
  TL civilization) is argued to be a probable co-evolutionary outcome once oxygenic
  photosynthesis starts, given sufficient time and a stable HZ orbit.
- **Limitation:** The paper is primarily a conceptual reassessment, not a quantitative
  Bayesian probability calculation. Numerical estimates for p(intelligence) are not
  provided.
- **Limitation:** "Probable outcome" is relative to the right planetary conditions being
  met (HZ orbit, liquid water, appropriate elemental abundances, stable star). The paper
  doesn't claim intelligence is common in an absolute sense — only that it is more
  probable than the hard-steps model predicts given those conditions.

## StarGen applicability
Critical update to the `ForganRice2010.txt` Rare Earth probability model. Mills et al.
argue that several of the hard steps in the Rare Earth model (especially the O₂ atmosphere
criterion, criterion 7) should be understood as co-evolutionary inevitabilities rather
than independent improbable accidents. This means:

1. If a planet is in the HZ, has liquid water, and develops photosynthetic life,
   the oxygen accumulation to Balbi2023 levels (pO₂ ≥ 18%) may be *more likely*
   than the ForganRice2010 default probability of ~10%
2. Multiple evolutionary "hard steps" in the ForganRice2010 model may have higher
   probabilities than the paper's default estimates
3. The "Optimistic" CivilizationEmergenceModel in ForganRice2010 may be better
   supported by Mills2024 than the Rare Earth "Conservative" model

The net effect for StarGen: **raise the probability for the Optimistic model variant;
keep the Rare Earth variant for pessimists.** This is a scientifically grounded basis
for the user-tunable CivilizationEmergenceModel enum.

```
Step                          | Carter/Hanson | Mills2024 revision
------------------------------|---------------|-------------------
Abiogenesis (origin of life)  | Hard          | Still hard (uncertain)
Oxygenic photosynthesis       | Moderate      | Easier (1 event → inevitable given time)
GOE (O₂ accumulation)         | Hard          | NOT hard (co-evolutionary inevitability)
Eukaryotic cell               | Hard          | Moderately hard (fewer steps than thought)
Multicellularity              | Hard          | NOT hard (evolved 25+ times independently)
Sexual reproduction           | Moderate      | NOT hard (clear adaptive advantage)
Complex nervous system        | Hard          | Moderate (convergent evolution evidence)
Language/cognition            | Hard          | Still uncertain
```

**Probability update for ForganRice2010 criteria:**
```csharp
// Mills2024-informed probability adjustments to ForganRice2010 criteria:
// Criterion 7 (oxygen atmosphere) — under Mills2024 interpretation:
//   If complex photosynthetic life already exists on a HZ planet,
//   p_oxygen_threshold (reaching pO2 ≥ 18%) is raised to 0.40 (from 0.10)
//   because co-evolutionary dynamics make it more probable than an independent event.
const double P_OXYGEN_MILLS2024    = 0.40;  // vs. ForganRice REH value of 0.10
const double P_OXYGEN_FORGAN_REH   = 0.10;  // conservative (Rare Earth)
const double P_OXYGEN_OPTIMISTIC   = 0.60;  // fully optimistic

// Apply based on CivilizationEmergenceModel setting:
double pOxygen = settings.CivilizationEmergenceModel switch
{
    CivilizationEmergenceModel.RareEarth  => P_OXYGEN_FORGAN_REH,
    CivilizationEmergenceModel.Optimistic => P_OXYGEN_MILLS2024,   // Mills-informed optimism
    CivilizationEmergenceModel.VeryOptimistic => P_OXYGEN_OPTIMISTIC,
    _ => P_OXYGEN_FORGAN_REH
};
```

**Hard-steps model → user lever:**
`HardStepsModel` (enum: Carter1983 / Mills2024 / ForganRice2010)
- Carter1983: maximum pessimism; all steps independent and rare
- ForganRice2010: intermediate (the original paper's Rare Earth defaults)
- Mills2024: co-evolutionary model; fewer genuinely hard steps (default for StarGen
  because it produces more interesting civilized worlds)
