## Abstract
The Gaia mission has triggered major developments in the field of Galactic dynamics in
recent years, which we discuss in this review. The structure and kinematics of all Galactic
components — disc, bar/bulge, and halo — are now mapped in great detail not only in the
Solar neighbourhood, but across a large part of the Milky Way. The dramatic improvements
in the coverage and precision of observations revealed various disequilibrium processes,
such as perturbations in the Galactic disc and the deformations of the outer halo, which
are partly attributed to the interaction with satellite galaxies. The knowledge of the
gravitational potential at all scales has also advanced considerably, but we are still far
from having a consistent view on the key properties of the Galaxy, such as the bar pattern
speed or the mass profile and shape of the dark halo. The complexity and interplay of
several dynamical processes makes the interpretation of observational data challenging,
and it is fair to say that more theoretical effort is needed to fully reap the fruit of
the Gaia revolution.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Gaia revolution in Galactic dynamics:** Gaia provides full 6D phase-space information
  for billions of stars, enabling detailed mapping of disk, bar/bulge, and halo kinematics
  across the Galaxy — far beyond previous Solar-neighbourhood-limited studies.
- **Disk disequilibrium:** The Galactic disk is not in a static equilibrium. Gaia reveals
  phase-space spirals, moving groups, and corrugations in the disk — partly from
  satellite galaxy interactions (Sagittarius passage) and partly from internal resonances
  with the bar and spiral arms.
- **Outer halo deformations:** The Gaia halo shows significant substructure (stellar
  streams, shells, the "Gaia Sausage/Enceladus" merger remnant) and dynamical
  disequilibrium in the outer halo due to past and ongoing satellite galaxy accretion.
- **Bar pattern speed uncertain:** The Milky Way bar pattern speed is still debated.
  Estimates range from ~35 to ~55 km/s/kpc; Gaia has narrowed but not fully resolved
  this. The bar's corotation resonance location shifts significantly over this range.
- **Dark halo mass profile uncertain:** Total MW mass estimates from Gaia range from
  ~0.7×10¹² M⊙ to ~1.5×10¹² M⊙; the halo shape (oblate, prolate, triaxial) remains
  contested.
- **Limitation:** This is a review; specific quantitative values (pattern speed, halo mass,
  velocity dispersion) have large uncertainties spanning the literature. StarGen should
  treat these as ranges, not point estimates.
- **Limitation:** Gaia's kinematic mapping covers primarily the disk and nearby halo.
  The very outer halo (> 50 kpc) remains poorly constrained.
