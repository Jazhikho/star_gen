## Abstract
Chacua et al. discuss innovation policy through an economic-complexity lens. The paper
argues that development and technological progress are processes of accumulating and
diversifying productive knowledge. Effective innovation policy should therefore be
place-based and multidimensional: it should use existing capabilities, identify binding
constraints, and avoid copying policies designed for problems a society does not have.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Capability portfolio framing:** Regions and countries are treated as portfolios of
  capabilities and know-how, not as blank slates that can adopt any frontier technology
  at equal cost.
- **Path dependence:** Future technological options are shaped by existing productive
  capabilities, related industries, institutions, and learning channels.
- **Binding constraints:** Policy should target the constraints that prevent movement
  toward more complex activities, not only the activities that are currently adjacent.
- **Place-based design:** Copying another society's solution is weak if the local society
  does not share the same capability base or problem structure.
- **Technology diffusion channels:** Migration, foreign direct investment, education,
  and institutional support are treated as mechanisms that diffuse capabilities.
- **Limitation:** This is a policy and conceptual framework, not a directly calibrated
  settlement or government generator. StarGen should not convert it into fixed numeric
  coefficients without a separate human-reviewed modeling step.

## StarGen applicability
Useful for StarGen's sentient-world and civilization layer, especially for technology
adoption, economic complexity, trade specialization, and industrial-development pressure.
It supports a model where a world's future technology and trade profile depends on the
capabilities it already has rather than on a simple linear tech-level ladder.

Provides:
- A capability-based rationale for path-dependent `TechnologyAdoptionCapacity`
- A reason to separate "frontier technology availability" from "local adoption readiness"
- Support for trade and migration as capability-transfer channels
- A warning against forcing every advanced world toward the same industrial profile

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Capability portfolio state:**
```
technology_adoption_capacity =
    f(existing_capabilities, relatedness, trade_connectivity, institutions, constraints)
```

**Generator rule implication:**
- A world with high trade connectivity but low local capability should receive import
  access, not automatically high local production capacity.
- Worlds should gain technology most easily in fields related to their existing resource,
  industrial, scientific, or institutional base.
- Policy/government output should distinguish "can import/use" from "can invent/produce".

**Potential StarGen fields:**
- `CapabilityDiversity`
- `CapabilityRelatedness`
- `BindingConstraintPressure`
- `KnowledgeTransferAccess`
- `TechnologyAdoptionCapacity`
