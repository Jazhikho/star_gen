## Abstract
We present an analysis of a volume-complete sample of 363 mid-to-late M dwarfs within 15 pc of the Sun with masses between 0.1-0.3 M observed by TESS within Sectors 1 to 42. The median stellar mass of the sample is 0.17 M . We search the TESS light curves for transiting planets with orbital periods below 7 days and recover all 6 known planets within the sample as well as a likely planet candidate orbiting LHS 475. Each of these planets is consistent with a terrestrial composition, with planet radii between 0.91-1.31 R⊕ . We characterize the transit detection sensitivity for each star as a function of planet radius, insolation, and orbital period. We obtain a cumulative occurrence rate of 0.61+0.24 −0.19 terrestrial planets per star with radii above 0.5 R⊕ and orbital periods between 0.4-7 days. We find that for comparable insolations, planets larger than 1.5 R⊕ (sub-Neptunes) are significantly less abundant around mid-to-late M dwarfs compared to earlier-type stars, while the occurrence rate of terrestrial planets is comparable to that of more massive M dwarfs. We estimate that overall, terrestrials outnumber sub-Neptunes around mid-to-late M dwarfs by 14 to 1, in contrast to GK dwarfs where they are roughly equinumerous.
