## Abstract
Hamilton et al. analyze more than one thousand human societies across levels of
sociopolitical complexity. They find self-similar scaling across jurisdictional hierarchy:
adjacent levels show roughly a four-fold increase in population, a two-fold increase in
geographic range, and a doubling of population density. The paper also stresses that
population metrics overlap across complexity levels, so large or dense populations do
not automatically imply high complexity.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Jurisdictional hierarchy scale:** Sociopolitical complexity is represented as levels
  of jurisdictional hierarchy beyond local community.
- **Population scaling:** Adjacent complexity levels are associated with approximately
  four-fold population increases.
- **Range scaling:** Adjacent levels are associated with approximately two-fold
  geographic-range increases.
- **Density scaling:** Population density approximately doubles across complexity levels.
- **Overlap warning:** Larger and denser societies tend to be more complex, but large
  population and density are not sufficient conditions for complexity.
- **Limitation:** This is human historical/anthropological data. It should inform
  human-audited sentient-world baselines, not be treated as a universal law for all
  alien societies.

## StarGen applicability
Useful for StarGen's sentient-world baseline, especially `SocialScale`,
`StateCapacity`, settlement rank, factional hierarchy, and government complexity. It
supports making population an important driver while preserving the rule that population
alone cannot determine social complexity.

Provides:
- A source anchor for population and density as sociopolitical-complexity pressure
- Support for hierarchical settlement/government scaling
- A caution that complexity must also depend on administration, trade, surplus, conflict,
  and institutional capacity
