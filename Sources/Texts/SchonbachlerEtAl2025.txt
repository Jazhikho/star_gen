## Abstract
This review explores the timescales of the initial phase of planet formation, from nebular
dust (CAIs and chondrules) to planetesimal accretion and differentiation, using evidence
from meteorite research. Aluminium-Mg systematics of CAIs indicate either an extended
period of CAI formation (~0.3 Ma) or initial ²⁶Al heterogeneity, with evidence supporting
a homogeneous ²⁶Al abundance in the protoplanetary disk. Thermal and aqueous alteration
on the parent body can disturb the U-Pb and Al-Mg chronometers in chondrules. Focusing
on relatively robust isochron data from plagioclase of pristine chondrites indicates a shift
in chondrule formation locations, moving from the inner to the outer disk over time. Ages of
basaltic achondrites show that silicate differentiation on small bodies was well underway
within the first few million years (Ma) of our solar system. Nucleosynthetic isotope data
indicate that parent bodies of iron and stony meteorites formed in two distinct regions:
the inner, non-carbonaceous (NC) and outer, carbonaceous (CC) region. Based on Hf-W
chronometry, NC and CC parent bodies of magmatic iron meteorites segregated their cores
within ~1–3 Ma after CAI formation, implying parent body accretion within < 1 Ma in both
reservoirs. NC and CC reservoirs were established within 1 Ma and persisted over several Ma
with limited exchange between them.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **CAI formation marks t₀ of solar system:** Calcium-aluminium-rich inclusions (CAIs) are
  the oldest solar system solids (~4.567 Ga). CAI formation occupied ~0.3 Ma. These set the
  absolute time zero for all solar system dating.
- **Chondrule formation timeline:** Chondrules (the main component of chondrites, up to 80%)
  formed from ~1–3 Ma after CAIs, with formation locations shifting from inner to outer disk
  over time. The protoplanetary disk lifetime is ~1–5 Ma (consistent with ALMA observations).
- **Planetesimal accretion: < 1 Ma:** Both NC (inner, dry) and CC (outer, wet/icy) parent
  bodies accreted within < 1 Ma after CAI formation. Core segregation (metal-silicate
  differentiation) occurred within ~1–3 Ma.
- **Two-reservoir structure (NC vs. CC):** The protoplanetary disk had two isotopically
  distinct reservoirs from early on: inner (NC = non-carbonaceous, volatile-poor) and outer
  (CC = carbonaceous, volatile-rich). These were separated for several Ma with limited mixing.
  Implication: inner rocky planets and outer ice/volatile-rich bodies have fundamentally
  different primordial compositions.
- **²⁶Al as heat source:** Radioactive decay of ²⁶Al (half-life 0.705 Ma) was the primary
  heat source for early planetesimal differentiation. Bodies accreting early (< 1 Ma after
  CAIs) had enough ²⁶Al to melt and differentiate; bodies accreting late (> 2 Ma) were too
  cold and remained undifferentiated chondrites.
- **Volatile delivery to rocky planets:** CC material (outer disk, carbonaceous) is the
  source of water and volatiles delivered to inner planets via impactors (pebble accretion
  or giant planet migration sculpting). The NC/CC boundary corresponds to the original
  snow line position.
- **Limitation:** Model assumes Solar System is representative of planetary systems generally.
  Protoplanetary disk lifetimes, snow line positions, and NC/CC ratios depend on stellar
  mass and disk chemistry — these may vary substantially around other star types.
- **Limitation:** Meteorite record samples only our own solar system. Results may not
  generalize; extrapolation to exoplanetary systems requires theoretical modeling.

## StarGen applicability
Provides the **protoplanetary disk and planetesimal timescale model** for StarGen's planet
formation layer. Specifically:
1. Establishes disk lifetime (~1–5 Ma) as the window for planet assembly
2. Quantifies the NC/CC reservoir dichotomy — inner rocky vs. outer volatile-rich
   composition as a function of snow line position
3. ²⁶Al-driven early differentiation: planetesimals that form early melt and differentiate;
   late ones remain primitive (affects rocky planet iron-core mass fraction)
4. Volatile delivery to inner planets is stochastic — depends on CC impactor flux crossing
   the snow line (Jupiter gap formation timing)

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**Protoplanetary disk timescales:**
```
Event                        | Timing after CAIs (t₀)
-----------------------------|------------------------
CAI formation                | 0 – 0.3 Ma
Chondrule formation          | 1 – 3 Ma
Planetesimal accretion       | < 1 Ma (NC and CC)
Core segregation             | 1 – 3 Ma
NC/CC reservoir separation   | < 1 Ma
Disk dispersal (gas phase)   | ~3 – 5 Ma
Terrestrial planet assembly  | ~10 – 100 Ma (after disk dispersal)
```

**NC/CC composition dichotomy (snow line basis):**
```csharp
// Planet bulk composition depends on formation location relative to snow line
// Inner disk (< snow line): NC composition — rocky, volatile-poor, high iron fraction
// Outer disk (> snow line): CC composition — volatile-rich, water/ice-bearing
bool innerDisk = planet.SemiMajorAuAtFormation < star.SnowLineAu;

if (innerDisk)
{
    planet.CompositionClass = CompositionClass.NonCarbonaceous;  // rocky, volatile-poor
    planet.WaterMassFraction = rng.NextDouble() * 0.01;          // 0-1% water (delivered later)
    planet.IronCoreFraction = 0.25 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.15;   // Earth-like iron core
}
else
{
    planet.CompositionClass = CompositionClass.Carbonaceous;     // volatile-rich
    planet.WaterMassFraction = 0.05 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.20;  // 5-25% water by mass
    planet.IronCoreFraction = 0.10 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.10;   // smaller iron core
}
```

**²⁶Al differentiation timing (affects iron core development):**
```csharp
// Early-accreting planetesimals fully differentiated; late ones remain chondritic
// This affects the iron core fraction of rocky planets
double accretionTimeMa = /* estimate from disk model */;
if (accretionTimeMa < 1.0)
{
    // Early accretion: ²⁶Al drives full melting → large iron core
    planet.IronCoreFraction *= 1.2;
}
else if (accretionTimeMa > 2.5)
{
    // Late accretion: ²⁶Al mostly decayed → limited differentiation
    planet.IronCoreFraction *= 0.7;
}
```

**Snow line scaling with stellar luminosity:**
```csharp
// Snow line position scales with stellar luminosity
// Snow line AU ≈ 2.7 × (L_star / L_sun)^0.5 (approximate)
double snowLineAu = 2.7 * Math.Sqrt(star.LuminositySolar);
star.SnowLineAu = snowLineAu;
```

**Related corpus papers:**

**Papers in this topical collection (Space Science Reviews 221, 2025):**
This paper is part of a larger collection on early solar system formation — other papers
in the same collection may also be relevant to StarGen (e.g., Lourenço et al. on plate
tectonics initiation; Baumeister et al. on interior structure of exoplanets).

**Key limitation for exoplanet application:**
Schönbächler et al. use meteoritic data from our Solar System only. Extension to other
stellar systems requires the disk chemistry and stellar mass to be similar. Around M dwarfs,
the snow line is closer (lower luminosity), and the NC/CC ratio may differ. Around A/F
stars, the disk is more energetic and volatile-poor in general. StarGen should scale snow
line position by stellar luminosity (see formula above) rather than using a fixed 2.7 AU.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### NC/CC snow line boundary for planet composition
SchonbachlerEtAl2025 establishes the physical basis for the inner-dry / outer-wet planet
compositional dichotomy used in StarGen's planet generator. The key mechanic is:
- Snow line position = 2.7 × √(L_star/L_sun) AU
- Planets forming inward of snow line: rocky/dry (NC class)
- Planets forming outward: water-rich (CC class), with stochastic volatile delivery
  to inner planets via impactor flux

**Primary update to StarGen planet generator:**
1. Compute snow line position during system generation (per stellar luminosity)
2. Assign NC vs. CC composition class based on formation location
3. Apply ²⁶Al differentiation timing modifier to iron core fraction
4. For NC inner planets: water delivery is stochastic (0–1% base; modified by
   Jupiter-equivalent presence and migration history)
