## Abstract
We present the deepest systematic search for planets around mid-to-late M dwarfs to date.
We have surveyed 8134 mid-to-late M dwarfs observed by TESS with a custom built pipeline
and recover 77 vetted transiting planet candidates. We characterize the sensitivity of our
survey via injection-recovery and measure the occurrence rate of planets as a function of
orbital period, instellation, and planet radius. We measure a cumulative occurrence rate of
1.10 ± 0.16 planets per star with radii > 1 R⊕ orbiting within 30 days. This value is
consistent with the cumulative occurrence rate around early M dwarfs, making M dwarfs
collectively the most prolific hosts of small close-in planets. Unlike the bimodal Radius
Valley exhibited by close-in planet population around FGK and early M dwarfs, we recover
a unimodal planet radius distribution peaking at 1.25 ± 0.05 R⊕. We additionally find
0.954 ± 0.147 super-Earths and 0.148 ± 0.045 sub-Neptunes per star, with super-Earths
outnumbering sub-Neptunes 5.5:1, firmly demonstrating that the Radius Valley disappears
around the lowest mass stars. The dearth of sub-Neptunes around mid-to-late M dwarfs is
consistent with predictions from water-rich pebble accretion models that predict a fading
Radius Valley with decreasing stellar mass. Our results support the emerging idea that the
sub-Neptune population around M dwarfs is composed of water-rich worlds. We find no hot
Jupiters in our survey and set an upper limit of 0.012 hot Jupiters per mid-to-late M dwarf
within 10 days.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Radius Valley disappears around M4+ hosts:** The bimodal gap at ~1.7 R⊕ that
  separates super-Earths from sub-Neptunes around FGK and early M dwarfs is absent for
  mid-to-late M dwarfs. Instead, a unimodal distribution peaks at 1.25 ± 0.05 R⊕.
- **Occurrence rates (mid-to-late M dwarfs):**
  - Cumulative (radii > 1 R⊕, P < 30 d): 1.10 ± 0.16 planets/star
  - Super-Earths (1–2 R⊕ approx): 0.954 ± 0.147 per star
  - Sub-Neptunes (~2–4 R⊕ approx): 0.148 ± 0.045 per star
  - Super-Earth : sub-Neptune ratio: 5.5 : 1
  - Hot Jupiters (P < 10 d): < 0.012 per star (upper limit)
- **Water-rich composition model:** The absence of the radius valley is consistent with
  water-rich pebble accretion models (Burn et al. 2024): around low-mass M dwarfs,
  planets form water-rich ("ocean worlds") rather than rocky+H/He worlds. The sub-Neptune
  population is therefore water-rich, not gas-envelope-stripped rocky planets.
- **Formation mechanism implication:** The thermal photoevaporation and core-powered mass
  loss models that explain the radius valley around FGK stars break down for M4+ dwarfs
  because the planet formation environment (pebble-dominated, water-ice-rich) is
  fundamentally different.
- **Consistency with SolizWelsh2026:** Water-rich worlds around late M dwarfs would not
  accumulate atmospheric oxygen via oxygenic photosynthesis in the same way rocky land-
  bearing worlds do. This is consistent with SolizWelsh2026's finding that complex life
  (and GOE-like oxygen accumulation) cannot occur around late M-dwarfs.
- **Sample:** 8,134 mid-to-late M dwarfs from TESS, 77 vetted transit candidates.
  Survey sensitivity characterized via injection-recovery tests.
- **Limitation:** Radii are photometric; composition (water vs. rock) inferred from
  formation models, not direct measurement. Actual composition requires mass measurements.
- **Limitation:** Survey covers P < 30 days; occurrence rates at longer periods
  (and in the habitable zone) are unconstrained.
- **Limitation:** First author name garbled in text extraction; verify APA citation.

**StarGen radius gap switch:**
```csharp
// Radius Valley model selection by host star type (Gillis et al. 2026)
bool radiusValleyEnabled = star.SpectralType switch
{
    SpectralType.M when star.TemperatureK < 3500 => false,  // M4+: unimodal
    SpectralType.M when star.TemperatureK < 3850 => false,  // M2-M4: borderline; disable
    _ => true  // FGK, early M: bimodal radius valley active
};

// Planet radius distribution for mid-to-late M dwarfs
// Unimodal: log-normal centered at 1.25 R⊕ (σ ~ 0.25 R⊕)
double peakRadiusEarth = 1.25;
double radiusSigma = 0.25;
if (!radiusValleyEnabled)
{
    planet.RadiusEarth = MathHelper.LogNormalSample(rng, peakRadiusEarth, radiusSigma);
}
```

**Sub-Neptune composition bias for M4+ hosts:**
```csharp
// For M4+ host stars: sub-Neptunes are water-rich, not gas-envelope rocky
if (!radiusValleyEnabled && planet.RadiusEarth >= 1.5 && planet.RadiusEarth <= 2.5)
{
    planet.CompositionModel = CompositionModel.WaterRich;
    planet.HasPrimaryAtmosphere = false;  // pebble accretion water world
    planet.OceanFraction = 0.7 + rng.NextDouble() * 0.3;  // 70-100% ocean coverage
}
```

**Hot Jupiter suppression for M4+ hosts:**
```csharp
// Hot Jupiter probability upper limit from Gillis et al. survey
double hotJupiterProbability = star.SpectralType == SpectralType.M
    && star.TemperatureK < 3500
    ? 0.012  // hard upper limit from survey
    : defaultHotJupiterProbability;
```

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Radius Valley disappears around M4+ dwarfs
GillisEtAl2026 establishes that the planet size distribution around mid-to-late M dwarfs
is fundamentally different from that around FGK stars. The standard bimodal radius
valley (used in most current planet generation codes) should be suppressed for M4+ hosts.

**Primary update to StarGen planet generation:**
1. Add stellar-type check before drawing from radius distribution
2. Use unimodal distribution (peak 1.25 R⊕, σ ~ 0.25) for M4+ hosts
3. Mark sub-Neptune planets around M4+ hosts as water-rich (ocean world composition)
4. Apply hot Jupiter probability cap of 0.012 for M4+ hosts

**Interaction with SolizWelsh2026 (M-dwarf habitability):**
The combination of Gillis (ocean worlds dominate) + SolizWelsh (PAR too low for
GOE) + LugerBarnes (XUV strips hydrogen) forms a coherent picture in which M4+
exoplanets in the habitable zone are predominantly water-covered, oxygen-depleted,
and unable to sustain complex life — but may support microbial life in subsurface oceans
(analogous to Enceladus or Europa in the Solar System).

Ribas2015.txt (XUV evolution), ForganRice2010.txt (habitable planet criteria).
