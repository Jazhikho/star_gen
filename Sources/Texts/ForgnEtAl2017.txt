## Abstract
This paper evaluates the Galactic Habitable Zone (GHZ) using a high-resolution
cosmological simulation of galaxy formation, specifically the CLUES (Constrained Local
UniversE Simulations) realization of a Local Group analog. Unlike analytic models of
the GHZ (e.g., Lineweaver et al. 2004) that assume steady-state galaxy properties, this
approach captures the time-dependent nature of the GHZ as the Milky Way-like galaxy
forms and evolves. The GHZ is modeled as a spatially and temporally varying probability
distribution governed by three factors: (1) sufficient metallicity for rocky planet
formation ([Fe/H] > threshold), (2) sufficiently low supernova rate to avoid sterilizing
radiation, and (3) sufficient elapsed time for complex life to evolve (>4 Gyr window).
The paper also introduces a "soft" habitability formalism using continuous probability
weighting rather than binary in/out boundaries, and discusses how the GHZ changes over
cosmic time.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- GHZ is NOT a static ring: it evolves substantially with time as the galaxy forms
- At early epochs the GHZ is nearly absent (insufficient metallicity everywhere)
- At intermediate times (~4–6 Gyr ago) a ring begins to form in the inner/middle disk
- At present day: GHZ is an annulus at approximately 4–10 kpc from galactic center
  (inner boundary set by SN rate; outer boundary set by metallicity threshold)
- The peak of the GHZ probability distribution shifts slightly inward over time
- Soft habitability produces a more extended, gradual GHZ boundary vs hard threshold
- Time window for complex life: at least 4 Gyr required → sets when in the GHZ history
  habitability "turns on"
- Metallicity threshold: [Fe/H] ≥ −0.5 dex required for sufficient rocky-planet
  formation material (approximate; paper explores threshold sensitivity)
- SN sterilization rate: based on Galactic supernova history from the CLUES simulation
- Limitation: single simulation realization; scatter between simulations not quantified
- Limitation: SN sterilization model is simplified; does not model GRBs or AGN flares
- Limitation: "complex life" = 4 Gyr assumption is anthropocentric and uncertain
- The soft habitability formalism is the paper's key methodological contribution;
  allows GHZ to be treated as a probability field rather than a sharp boundary

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**GHZ spatial extent (present-day Milky Way-analog):**
- Inner edge: ~4 kpc (driven by SN sterilization rate)
- Outer edge: ~10 kpc (driven by metallicity threshold)
- Width: ~6 kpc in the hard-boundary case; the soft boundary extends this to ~3–13 kpc
  with graded probability weighting
- Transition width: ~1–2 kpc for a soft boundary in the inner/outer zone (σ of the
  probability ramp)

**The three GHZ gates (and their StarGen lever mapping):**
1. **Metallicity gate:** [Fe/H] ≥ −0.5 dex for rocky planet formation
   → parameterised by `metallicity_gradient_dex_per_kpc` and central [Fe/H]
2. **SN sterilization gate:** local SN rate < threshold (empirically: outer > 4 kpc from
   center in MW analogs is typically safe at present day)
   → parameterised implicitly by `ghz_inner_radius_pc`
3. **Time window gate:** host star age > 4 Gyr (for complex life) or > ~0.5 Gyr (for
   simple life); this is the temporal gate
   → parameterised by `ghz_transition_width_pc` indirectly; expose as separate lever

**Soft vs hard habitability toggle:**
- Hard: binary in/out; GHZ is a sharp annulus
- Soft: probability p(r) = f(metallicity, SN rate, age) × continuous; results in a
  gradual falloff toward GHZ boundaries
- The soft formalism produces a broader GHZ; the hard formalism is conservative

**Time evolution:**
- GHZ did not exist as an annulus before ~8 Gyr ago (too metal-poor everywhere, or
  too high SN rate)
- For generating stars in a galaxy at lookback time t, the GHZ boundaries shift:
  inner boundary moves outward (higher SN rate at earlier times); outer boundary
  moves inward (less metallicity at earlier times) → net result: smaller GHZ in the past

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**Context and companion papers:**
- Lineweaver, C. H., Fenner, Y., & Gibson, B. K. (2004) — the original GHZ paper
  (Science 303, 59); analytic model that Forgan et al. extend/supersede. ADS reference
  for comparison.
- SpitoniEtAl2017.txt (in corpus) — chemical-evolution companion; provides metallicity
  gradient vs time that feeds into the GHZ metallicity gate.

**Follow-up work on GHZ:**
- Gobat, R., & Hong, S. E. (2016) — GHZ in early-type galaxies; GHZ may not exist in
  ellipticals (no SFR gradient; no SN ring boundary).
- Zackrisson, E., et al. (2016) — GHZ across different universe realizations
  (arXiv:1612.01864); examines whether Milky Way is typical.
- Carigi, L., García-Rojas, J., & Montiel, A. (2013) — chemical evolution constraints
  on GHZ for the MW.
- Morrison, P. R., & Gowanlock, M. G. (2015) — updated GHZ model with improved SN
  sterilization estimates; suggests GHZ is larger than Lineweaver (2004) estimated.
- **As of 2024:** JWST and improved galactic chemical evolution models (e.g., APOGEE
  constraints; see HackshawEtAl2024.txt, TrentinEtAl2024.txt) have tightened the
  metallicity gradient inputs to the GHZ model. The 4–10 kpc boundary estimate remains
  broadly consistent with updated inputs.

**Criticism and alternatives:**
- Prantzos, N. (2008) — argues GHZ boundaries are too uncertain to be useful; entire
  MW disk may be habitable if metallicity threshold is relaxed.
- The anthropocentric 4 Gyr time window is a significant assumption; relaxing it
  expands the GHZ substantially.

**Citation discovery:**
- ADS: https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017IJAsB..16...60F

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Galactic Habitable Zone model
This paper (with SpitoniEtAl2017) provides the GHZ parameter basis in StarGen's galaxy
generator. Both are cited in GalaxyScienceReferenceCatalog.cs.

**GHZModel selector:**
- `Forgan2017Soft` (default for HardSF/Realistic): continuous probability weighting
  using three gates; GHZ appears as a gradual probability ramp.
- `Lineweaver2004Hard`: binary annulus (historic reference model; deprecated for
  scientific use but useful for comparison).
- `NoBoundary`: no GHZ applied (SpaceOpera mode; habitability decided by other rules).

**Default parameter values (Milky Way-like galaxy at present day):**
- `ghz_inner_radius_pc`: 4,000 pc (4 kpc); driven by SN sterilization rate
- `ghz_outer_radius_pc`: 10,000 pc (10 kpc); driven by metallicity threshold
- `ghz_transition_width_pc`: 1,000 pc (1 kpc) — width of the soft probability ramp
  at each boundary (half-Gaussian σ)

**Three-gate probability computation:**
```
p_metallicity = logistic([Fe/H](r) - threshold_FeH, steepness)
p_sn_safe    = logistic(r - r_inner_hard, transition_kpc)
p_time       = 1 if star_age > min_complex_life_gyr else 0  (hard gate)
p_ghz        = p_metallicity × p_sn_safe × p_time
```
- `threshold_FeH`: default −0.5 dex; expose as user lever `GHZMetallicityThreshold`
- `min_complex_life_gyr`: default 4.0 Gyr; expose as `GHZComplexLifeTimeGyr`

**Time evolution hook:**
For galaxy generator tools that simulate at a lookback time, scale `ghz_inner_radius_pc`
upward and `ghz_outer_radius_pc` downward proportionally to the metallicity and SN
history of the galaxy at that epoch (implement as a scaling factor table vs lookback time).

**User levers:** `GHZModel`, `GHZMetallicityThreshold`, `GHZComplexLifeTimeGyr`,
`GHZTransitionWidthPc`.

**Cross-references:** SpitoniEtAl2017.txt (chemical evolution / metallicity gate),
HackshawEtAl2024.txt (radial metallicity gradient), TrentinEtAl2024.txt (Cepheid
metallicity gradient cross-check), WordsworthKreidberg2022.txt (atmosphere retention
at the individual planet level).
