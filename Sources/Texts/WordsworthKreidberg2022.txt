Source note for StarGen research review

Citation:
Wordsworth, R., & Kreidberg, L. (2022).
Atmospheres of rocky exoplanets.
Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics, 60, 159-201.
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.04663
PDF: https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~rwordsworth/papers/wordsworth2022atmospheres.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-astro-052920-125632

What was reviewed:
- Abstract, review summary, and source metadata on 2026-04-19.

Key implementation notes used in StarGen:
- Many rocky planets likely form with early H2-He envelopes that can later be lost through stellar UV/X-ray irradiation and internal heating.
- Rocky-planet atmospheres should be treated as outcomes of flux, escape, volatile inventory, and interior exchange, not as decorative labels disconnected from the rest of the world state.
- Airless rocky planets are a real expected outcome, and atmospheric retention strongly constrains what surface conditions can stay stable.

How StarGen uses this:
- As reviewed support for Object Studio conflict notes when users force stripped or airless planets while also targeting dense pressure or ocean-heavy surface conditions.
- As a plain-language help source explaining why direct atmosphere, hydrosphere, and envelope locks can pull against each other even though Object Studio still honors those direct choices.
