## Abstract
Gravitational interaction between a planet and a three-dimensional isothermal gaseous
disk is studied. In the present paper we mainly examine the torque on a planet and the
resultant radial migration of the planet. A planet excites density waves at Lindblad and
corotation resonances and experiences a negative torque by the density waves, which
causes a rapid inward migration of the planet during its formation in a protoplanetary
disk. We formulate the linear wave excitation in three-dimensional isothermal disks and
calculate the torques of Lindblad resonances and corotation resonances. For corotation
resonances, a general torque formula is newly derived. The net torque of the inner and
outer Lindblad resonances (the differential Lindblad torque) is caused by asymmetry such
as the radial pressure gradient and the scale height variation. In three-dimensional
disks, the differential Lindblad torques are generally smaller than in two-dimensional
disks. As a result, the migration time of a planet is of the order of 10⁶ yr for an
Earth-size planet at 5 AU for a typical disk model, which is longer than the 2D result
by a factor of 2 or 3.

## Major claims, findings, models, assumptions, and limitations
- **Canonical Type I migration timescale:** τ_mig ~ 10⁶ yr for a 1 M_Earth planet at 5 AU
  in a typical disk model (surface density Σ ~ 10² g/cm² at 1 AU, h/r ~ 0.05)
- **3D correction factor:** The 3D torque calculation gives migration times 2–3× *longer*
  than the earlier 2D formula of Ward (1997) / Goldreich & Tremaine (1980). This is the
  canonical 3D Tanaka formula.
- **TTW torque formula (the key result):**
  τ_mig = (M_star / M_planet) × (M_star / Σ_g a²) × (h/a)² × Ω⁻¹ × C
  where C is a numerical coefficient from the 3D torque calculation (~2.7 for typical
  disk profiles; varies with surface density gradient α and flaring index β)
- **Total torque = Lindblad torque + corotation torque.** For isothermal disks the
  corotation torque is partially saturated; in the isothermal limit the net migration
  is dominated by the Lindblad torque and is always inward for typical disk profiles.
- **Mass scaling:** τ_mig ∝ M_planet⁻¹. A 10 M_Earth super-Earth migrates 10× faster
  than an Earth-mass planet. This makes Type I migration particularly dangerous for
  super-Earths forming beyond ~1 AU.
- **Disk mass scaling:** τ_mig ∝ Σ_g⁻¹. Heavier disks produce faster migration.
  Migration stalls or reverses if the disk is depleted (photoevaporation, gap opening).
- **Disk geometry:** Migration rate depends on the local disk aspect ratio h/r:
  τ_mig ∝ (h/r)². Hotter (flared) disks give slower migration.
- **Limitation (critical):** The paper assumes an *isothermal* disk. In reality,
  non-isothermal (radiative) disks produce a substantially different corotation torque
  that can be positive (outward) — this is the core correction by Paardekooper et al.
  (2011) and Jiménez & Masset (2017). The isothermal TTW formula systematically
  overestimates inward migration rate in radiative disks.
- **Limitation:** Linear theory applies for M_planet < M_gap (gap-opening mass). Beyond
  this mass, the planet opens a gap and enters Type II migration (slower, locked to
  viscous disk evolution). Gap-opening mass ~ (h/r)³ × M_star / 3 ~ 10–100 M_Earth
  for typical conditions.
- **Limitation:** This is a single-planet calculation; multi-planet resonant trapping
  (e.g., mean-motion resonances) can halt or significantly modify migration chains.

## StarGen applicability
Canonical reference for Type I migration timescales in planet formation. Motivates the
architecture of compact inner-planet systems in StarGen: rocky planets at short orbital
periods most likely formed beyond the ice line and migrated inward during the gas disk
phase. The TTW formula sets the characteristic migration timescale for the initial
condition generator.

In StarGen, Type I migration is not simulated in real time (initial conditions only),
but the TTW timescale determines:
1. Whether a rocky planet at a given location plausibly migrated from beyond the ice line
2. The probability that small planets at <0.5 AU represent migrated super-Earths vs
   in-situ formation (which requires a high-density inner disk — see Raymond et al. 2018)
3. Architecture biases toward compact multi-planet systems (peas-in-a-pod) as post-
   migration outcomes

## Parameters, constraints, and mechanics implications

**TTW migration timescale formula (implementable):**
```
τ_mig (yr) ≈ 1.6 × 10⁶ × (M_star / M_sun)
                         × (M_planet / M_Earth)⁻¹
                         × (Σ_g(a) / 1700 g/cm²)⁻¹   [MMSN at 1 AU]
                         × (h/r / 0.05)²
                         × (a / 1 AU)^(α + β - 0.5)
where α = surface density slope, β = temperature slope (MMSN: α=1.5, β=0.5)
```
Simplified for code: τ_mig ~ 10⁶ yr × (M_planet / M_Earth)⁻¹ at 5 AU in a MMSN disk.

**Migration gate for rocky planets (<0.5 AU):**
- If τ_mig < disk_lifetime (~3 Myr): planet has likely migrated significantly
- If τ_mig > disk_lifetime: planet is roughly in-situ
- For M_planet = 1 M_Earth: τ_mig at 1 AU ~ 3×10⁵ yr → migrated
- For M_planet = 0.1 M_Earth (Moon mass): τ_mig ~ 3×10⁶ yr → marginally in-situ

**Gap-opening mass (transition to Type II):**
- M_gap ~ 3 × (h/r)³ × M_star ~ 15–50 M_Earth for h/r = 0.03–0.05
- Planets above M_gap are treated as Type II migrators (slower)

**Non-isothermal correction (critical update):**
Use Paardekooper et al. (2011) entropy-driven corotation torque for realistic disks.
In a disk with an outward temperature gradient (normal), the corotation torque is
positive and can halt or reverse migration for planets in the ~1–20 M_Earth range.
This is the primary reason the Tanaka formula alone should NOT be used for super-Earth
migration in radiative disk models.

## Conflicts and follow-up work

**Critical update (non-isothermal corotation):**
- Paardekooper, S.-J., Baruteau, C., Crida, A., & Kley, W. (2010) MNRAS 401, 1950 —
  first full 3D non-isothermal migration study. Corotation torques can reverse migration.
  arXiv:0909.2255.
- Paardekooper, S.-J., Baruteau, C., & Kley, W. (2011) MNRAS 410, 293 —
  "A torque formula for non-isothermal Type I planetary migration." arXiv:1007.4964.
  **This is the modern replacement for the corotation component of the TTW formula.**
  The Lindblad torque in TTW remains the standard; only the corotation term needs update.

**Modern reviews and simulation context:**
- Baruteau, C., et al. (2014) "Planet-Disk Interactions and Early Evolution of Planetary
  Systems." Protostars and Planets VI, pp. 667–689. arXiv:1312.4293. Comprehensive review
  of Type I and II migration, including the corotation torque debate.
- Izidoro, A., et al. (2017) MNRAS 470, 1750 — migration chains and resonant trapping
  of super-Earths; shows how the peas-in-a-pod architecture arises from coupled migration.
  arXiv:1703.03634. If IzidoroEtAl2017.txt is in corpus, cross-reference.

**Competing in-situ formation models:**
- Raymond, S. N., & Cossou, C. (2014) MNRAS 440, L11 — argues that most short-period
  super-Earths formed in-situ from a dense inner disk, not by migration. Competing
  interpretation for the same observed architecture.
- Chiang, E., & Laughlin, G. (2013) MNRAS 431, 3444 — detailed in-situ model.
  arXiv:1211.1673.

**TTW formula still the standard Lindblad torque reference:**
As of 2024, the Tanaka et al. (2002) 3D Lindblad torque formula remains the canonical
reference for the Lindblad component. The corotation torque should be updated per
Paardekooper et al. (2011) for any radiative/non-isothermal disk model.

## StarGen mechanics translation

### Type I migration awareness in initial condition generation

**MigrationAwareness flag:**
StarGen generates initial conditions (post-disk-dispersal), not real-time dynamics.
Migration does not run. However, migration sets up the architecture we inherit.

**Generation rules for rocky planet placement:**
```csharp
// For each rocky planet with M_planet < M_gap:
// Compute migration timescale at its current orbit
double tauMig = ComputeTTWMigrationTime(mPlanetEarths, semiMajorAxisAU,
                                         diskMassScale, diskAspectRatio);
// If tauMig < disk lifetime (default 3 Myr): flag as likely migrated
bool likelyMigrated = tauMig < settings.DiskLifetimeYr;

// Consequence: short-period rocky planets (< 0.5 AU) that are likelyMigrated
// carry different composition priors (may have accreted beyond the ice line)
// than in-situ planets (dry, refractory-rich)
if (likelyMigrated && semiMajorAxisAU < 0.5)
{
    planet.MigrationFlag = MigrationFlag.TypeI_Inward;
    planet.WaterMassFractionPrior = WaterPrior.IceLineEnhanced; // wetter on average
}
```

**Architecture consequence (peas-in-a-pod):**
When generating compact multi-planet systems, the rice2023 / HeEtAl2020 spacing
uniformity arises naturally from migration chains where planets pile up near resonances.
The `MigrationMode` enum controls whether StarGen uses the migrated-architecture priors
or in-situ priors for inner-planet composition.

**User lever:** `GenerationSettings.MigrationMode` (enum: TTW_Isothermal /
Paardekooper_Radiative / InSitu)
- TTW_Isothermal: use TTW formula; more aggressive inward migration
- Paardekooper_Radiative: longer migration for super-Earths due to outward corotation
- InSitu: assume no significant migration; planets form close to observed orbits

**Cross-references:** IzidoroEtAl2017.txt (resonant trapping, migration chains),
RiceEtAl2023.txt (peas-in-a-pod spacing uniformity, post-migration outcome),
HeEtAl2020.txt (AMD stability, architectural uniformity),
LambrechtsJohansen2012.txt (pebble accretion, growth timescale).
